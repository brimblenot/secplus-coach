## Compliance (Domain 5)

Compliance means proving your organization meets its legal, regulatory, contractual, and internal security obligations — not just being secure, but documenting it. This lesson covers the major frameworks, their requirements, and what happens when you fail.

---

### Compliance ≠ security

**Compliance** is the process of meeting legally, contractually, or internally defined security obligations. It is not the same as being secure.

- A compliant org may still be insecure — frameworks set **minimums**, not maximums
- A secure org may not be compliant if it can't *demonstrate* controls in the required format
- Think of compliance as a **floor**, not a ceiling — the baseline from which a mature program is built

> Analogy: A building can pass a fire inspection (compliant) but still have one exit in an inconvenient place. Passing the inspection doesn't mean the building is optimally safe.

---

### Four categories of compliance obligations

Organizations face multiple overlapping compliance types simultaneously:

| Category | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Regulatory** | Laws enacted by governments; legal penalties for failure | HIPAA, GDPR, SOX, GLBA, FERPA, FISMA |
| **Contractual** | Security requirements written into business contracts | PCI DSS (merchant agreements), ISO 27001 certification demanded by customers |
| **Industry standards** | Voluntary standards adopted as de facto requirements | ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type 2 |
| **Internal policy** | Org's own rules: AUP, password policy, data classification | Acceptable Use Policy compliance |

Key regulations to know:
- **HIPAA** — **Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act**: safeguards for healthcare data
- **GDPR** — **General Data Protection Regulation**: EU resident personal data protection (applies to *any* org processing that data)
- **PCI DSS** — **Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard**: contractual (not law), enforced by card brands; noncompliance can revoke ability to accept cards
- **SOX** — **Sarbanes-Oxley Act**: financial reporting integrity for publicly traded companies
- **GLBA** — **Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act**: information security programs at financial institutions
- **FERPA** — **Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act**: student education records
- **FISMA** — **Federal Information Security Management Act**: security requirements for federal agencies; **FedRAMP** — **Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program**: extends these requirements to cloud providers serving federal agencies
- **ISO 27001** — international certification for a formal **ISMS** (Information Security Management System)
- **SOC 2 Type 2** — **Service Organization Control** report proving a vendor's security controls worked effectively over a defined time period; widely required by enterprise customers

> Analogy: A restaurant must comply with health codes (regulatory), franchise rules (contractual), and its own kitchen cleanliness standards (internal policy) — all at once.

---

### PCI DSS 12 requirements (organized in six categories)

These 12 requirements are the exam-testable core of PCI DSS:

| # | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 1 | Install/maintain firewall to protect cardholder data |
| 2 | No vendor-supplied default passwords |
| 3 | Protect stored cardholder data |
| 4 | Encrypt cardholder data in transit over public networks |
| 5 | Protect systems against malware; keep anti-malware updated |
| 6 | Develop and maintain secure systems and applications |
| 7 | Restrict cardholder data access by need-to-know |
| 8 | Assign a unique ID to each person with computer access |
| 9 | Restrict physical access to cardholder data |
| 10 | Track and monitor all access to network resources and cardholder data |
| 11 | Regularly test security systems and processes |
| 12 | Maintain an information security policy for all personnel |

---

### HIPAA Security Rule: three safeguard categories

**ePHI** — **electronic Protected Health Information**: any digitally stored or transmitted health data covered by HIPAA.

| Safeguard | Key examples |
|---|---|
| **Administrative** | Security management process, designated security official, workforce training, incident procedures, contingency plan, business associate contracts |
| **Physical** | Facility access controls, workstation use policies, device and media controls |
| **Technical** | Access controls, audit controls, integrity controls, authentication, transmission encryption |

> Analogy: Administrative = the hospital's rule book; Physical = locked doors and badge readers; Technical = login screens and encryption.

---

### Due care vs. due diligence

- **Due care** — taking *reasonable steps* to protect assets from foreseeable harm; implementing appropriate controls is due care
- **Due diligence** — going further: actively *testing, monitoring, and verifying* that controls work and that third parties are meeting their obligations

> Analogy: Due care is installing a smoke detector. Due diligence is testing the battery monthly and verifying your tenants didn't disable it.

---

### Compliance monitoring tools

**Continuous monitoring** uses automated tools to evaluate configurations against defined requirements in real time — not just at audit time.

- **CIS-CAT** — **Center for Internet Security Configuration Assessment Tool**: measures compliance with **CIS Benchmarks** (hardening configuration standards)
- **SCAP** — **Security Content Automation Protocol**: standardized language for expressing and measuring compliance requirements
- Cloud-native tools: **AWS Config**, **Azure Policy**, **Google Cloud Security Command Center** — continuously check cloud resource configs and alert on violations

---

### Penalties for noncompliance

| Framework | Penalty |
|---|---|
| **GDPR** | Up to 4% of annual global revenue or €20M, whichever is greater |
| **HIPAA** | $100–$50,000 per violation; max $1.9M/category/year; up to 10 years prison for intentional misuse |
| **PCI DSS** | Up to $100,000/month from card brands; loss of card processing rights |
| **SOX** | Up to 20 years prison for executives who knowingly certify false financial reports |

Beyond fines: reputational damage, stock price decline, and lost business relationships.

---

### Recap
- Compliance is a **floor**, not a ceiling — meeting requirements doesn't guarantee security
- The four obligation types are **regulatory, contractual, industry standards, and internal policy**
- PCI DSS has **12 requirements**; HIPAA Security Rule has **three safeguard categories** (administrative, physical, technical)
- **Due care** = implementing controls; **due diligence** = verifying they actually work
- GDPR and HIPAA carry the heaviest financial and criminal penalties

### Exam flags
- **HIPAA vs. GDPR penalties and scope** — know which applies to healthcare vs. EU personal data, and the fine structures
- **PCI DSS requirement numbers** — especially requirements 2 (no default passwords), 8 (unique IDs), and 11 (regular testing)
- **Due care vs. due diligence** — a classic compare/contrast question; know that due diligence requires *active verification*, not just implementation
