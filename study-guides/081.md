## Asset Management

Asset management is how an organization knows what it owns, what's running, and what needs protecting. Without it, vulnerability scanning, patch management, and incident response all operate blind.

---

### What counts as an asset

Assets fall into four categories — hardware, software, data, and cloud.

- **Hardware assets** — physical devices: servers, workstations, laptops, mobile devices, switches, routers, firewalls, WAPs, printers, storage arrays, IoT devices
- **Software assets** — licensed apps, operating systems, VM images, firmware, dev tools
- **Data assets** — databases, file shares, backup repositories, cloud storage holding org data
- **Cloud assets** — SaaS subscriptions, compute instances, storage buckets, accounts across AWS, Azure, GCP

---

### Asset inventory and discovery

An **asset inventory** is the comprehensive, up-to-date list of everything an organization owns or manages. Manual inventory breaks down at enterprise scale; automated discovery is required.

Two main discovery approaches:

| Method | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Agent-based discovery** — a lightweight software program installed on each managed device that reports hardware/software details back to the management platform | Agent installed on device; reports installed apps, running services, hardware specs, patch status | Managed endpoints needing deep detail |
| **Agentless discovery** — uses network scanning and remote management protocols to gather device info *without* installing software | Probe packets sent across the network; no installation required | Network devices, printers, appliances; also catches **unauthorized devices** that IT didn't know were connected |

**Nmap** — a network scanning tool that discovers active hosts, open ports, and services by sending probe packets. Commercial platforms like **Lansweeper**, **Ivanti IT Asset Management**, and **Qualys AssetView** do continuous automated discovery at scale.

Think of agentless discovery like a census taker walking the neighborhood knocking on doors — they find every house, including ones the city forgot to register.

---

### The CMDB

A **CMDB** (Configuration Management Database) — a repository that stores not just what assets exist, but the *relationships between them* (which apps run on which servers, which servers support which business processes, which users own which assets).

- Enables **impact analysis**: when a server goes down or a CVE drops, you immediately know which business processes and users are affected
- **ServiceNow** is the most widely deployed CMDB platform in enterprise
- Used by change management, incident management, and security operations

Think of a CMDB like a wiring diagram for the whole organization — not just a parts list, but a map of how everything connects.

---

### Asset classification

**Asset classification** assigns criticality and sensitivity ratings that drive what security controls each asset receives.

| Class | What it means | Example controls |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | Failure halts core operations or causes major financial loss | Strictest configs, frequent scanning |
| **Sensitive** | Stores/processes/transmits confidential data (customer info, financials, IP) | Enhanced monitoring, tight change control |
| **Regulated** | Subject to PCI DSS, HIPAA, or GDPR compliance requirements | Compliance-specific controls |
| **General** | Supporting functions; no sensitive data, not operationally critical | Standard baseline controls |

### Hardware lifecycle phases

Every physical asset passes through four phases:

1. **Procurement** — verify supply chain integrity (guard against counterfeit/tampered hardware); screen for security issues before acceptance
2. **Deployment** — apply security baseline *before* connecting to production; assign asset tag; register in inventory with owner
3. **Operations** — regular patching, vulnerability scanning, configuration management to detect **drift** (deviation from the approved security baseline)
4. **Retirement** — sanitize data; obtain **certificate of destruction** — a document from a third party confirming which assets were destroyed, how, and when — for auditable proof of proper disposal

### Software license management and end-of-life tracking

**SAM** (Software Asset Management) — the practice of tracking licensed software to enforce compliance and block unauthorized installs.

- Unauthorized software may come from untrusted sources, cracked builds, or have known vulnerabilities — a legal *and* security risk
- SAM integrates with **application allowlisting** — a control that permits *only* approved, licensed software to execute on managed endpoints, automatically blocking anything else

**End-of-life (EOL) / End-of-support (EOS)** — the date a vendor stops releasing security patches for a product. Any vulnerability found after that date is *permanently unpatched*.

- Organizations must track EOL dates and either migrate to supported alternatives or apply **compensating controls** — substitute security measures used when the ideal control isn't possible — such as network isolation, enhanced monitoring, and restrictive firewall rules

---

### BYOD and mobile device management

**BYOD** (Bring Your Own Device) — personally owned devices that access corporate data. The org doesn't own the hardware, but the data on it is still an org asset.

**MDM** (Mobile Device Management) — a platform that manages security on personal devices accessing corporate resources.

- Provides inventory visibility into enrolled devices
- Enforces minimum security requirements
- Enables **selective wipe** — removes only corporate data, leaving personal data untouched — when a device is lost or an employee departs

---

### Cloud asset management

Cloud resources can be created and destroyed in minutes by anyone with permissions — traditional point-in-time audits can't keep up.

- **Resource tagging** — attaching standardized metadata fields (owner, environment, data classification, cost center, application) to cloud resources so security policies, cost allocation, and compliance reports can be applied automatically based on those tags
- Native services — **AWS Config**, **Azure Resource Graph**, **Google Cloud Asset Inventory** — continuously discover all resources and flag unauthorized creation or policy deviations
- Lifecycle management must identify and terminate unused instances, unattached storage volumes, orphaned load balancers, and forgotten dev environments

---

### Physical tracking and data destruction

**Asset tags** — barcodes, QR codes, or **RFID tags** (radio-frequency chips that transmit an ID when scanned) affixed to devices for inventory audits and location tracking. Annual physical audits compare records against devices present, surfacing missing or undocumented assets.

Portable equipment (laptops, cameras, specialty tools) uses check-in/check-out tracking to maintain custody accountability.

At disposal, **NIST SP 800-88** (Guidelines for Media Sanitization) governs how data is destroyed:

| Method | How it works | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Clearing** | Software overwrites data; software recovery infeasible, physical recovery still possible | Lower-sensitivity data; asset reused internally |
| **Purging** | Hardware techniques — **cryptographic erasure** (destroying the encryption key so encrypted data is unreadable) or **degaussing** (using a strong magnetic field to scramble magnetic media) — render recovery infeasible even with lab equipment | Sensitive data before external transfer or reuse |
| **Destruction** | Physical shredding, disintegration, pulverization, melting, or incineration | Highest-sensitivity data; recovery must be impossible |

---

### Asset management and vulnerability management

Vulnerability scanners can only target systems they know exist. When a **CVE** (Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures entry — a publicly identified security flaw in a specific product) is published, an accurate software inventory lets teams instantly identify which systems are affected. Scan results feed *back* into the asset inventory to update software version info and track remediation — closing the loop between knowing what you have and fixing what's broken.

---

### Recap

- **You cannot protect what you do not know you have** — complete inventory is the foundation of all security programs
- The **CMDB** maps asset *relationships*, enabling impact analysis when incidents or vulnerabilities occur
- **EOL/EOS software** is permanently unpatched after the vendor cutoff; compensating controls or migration are required
- Data sanitization method (clearing → purging → destruction) must match the data's classification per **NIST SP 800-88**
- **Agent-based discovery** gives the deepest detail; **agentless** catches unauthorized devices too

### Exam flags

- Be able to distinguish **clearing vs. purging vs. destruction** — method, mechanism, and when each applies (NIST SP 800-88 context)
- Know what a **CMDB** stores and why relationship mapping matters for impact analysis
- Recognize **EOL/EOS** as a vulnerability management risk and know what compensating controls address it
