## Securing Wireless and Mobile (ID: 078, Domain 4)

Mobile devices and wireless networks are high-value attack surfaces because they're always moving, frequently on untrusted networks, and used for both work and personal tasks. This lesson covers how organizations control mobile devices through deployment models, management platforms, and technical security controls.

---

### Mobile device deployment models

The deployment model determines who owns the device — and therefore how much control IT has over it.

**BYOD** — Bring Your Own Device — is a model where employees use personally owned hardware to access corporate resources.
- Lowest cost to employer (no device purchase)
- Highest risk: IT has minimal control over a device the employee owns and configures personally
- Employees often resist enrolling in **MDM** (Mobile Device Management — the centralized platform for enforcing security policy on enrolled devices) due to privacy concerns
- Minimum controls: MDM enrollment, encrypted work container, selective remote wipe of corporate data only

**COPE** — Corporate-Owned, Personally Enabled — the company buys the device but allows personal use.
- IT has full management authority because the organization owns it
- MDM policy covers the entire device, not just a container
- Remote wipe can erase everything if the device is lost

**CYOD** — Choose Your Own Device — company buys the device, employee picks from a pre-approved list.
- Better hardware standardization than BYOD, some personal preference still allowed
- Organization retains ownership and full management authority

**COBO** — Corporate-Owned, Business Only — company-owned, zero personal use permitted.
- Most restrictive model; appropriate for sensitive roles or specialized field equipment

| Model | Who Buys | Personal Use | IT Control Level |
|-------|----------|--------------|-----------------|
| BYOD | Employee | Yes | Lowest |
| COPE | Employer | Yes | High |
| CYOD | Employer | Yes (approved devices) | High |
| COBO | Employer | No | Highest |

> Think of BYOD as borrowing a friend's car for a work errand: you can't control how it's maintained, and they might object to you putting a GPS tracker on it.

---

### Mobile Device Management (MDM)

**MDM** is the centralized platform organizations use to configure, secure, and monitor enrolled mobile devices. It enforces policy without requiring a technician to touch each device.

Key MDM capabilities:
- **Policy enforcement**: PIN complexity, biometric settings, screen-lock timeout, encryption status
- **Application management**: push/remove apps, block unauthorized installs
- **Configuration delivery**: email profiles, VPN settings, Wi-Fi certificates — all pushed remotely
- **Security detection**: flags **jailbroken/rooted** devices (devices whose OS security controls have been bypassed by the user), enforces minimum OS version
- **Remote wipe**: full factory reset OR selective wipe of corporate data only
- **Inventory/tracking**: enrolled device list showing OS version, apps, compliance status

> MDM is the remote control for every device in your fleet — one policy change can lock down 10,000 phones simultaneously.

---

### Mobile Application Management (MAM) and EMM

**MAM** — Mobile Application Management — secures specific apps rather than the whole device. It wraps corporate apps with enforced security policies, applies **DLP** (Data Loss Prevention — controls that stop sensitive data from leaving authorized channels) at the app level, and can wipe only corporate apps without touching personal data.

MAM is especially valuable in BYOD scenarios where employees resist full device management, because it protects corporate data without giving IT visibility into the whole device.

**EMM** — Enterprise Mobility Management — is the comprehensive framework that combines MDM, MAM, **MCM** (Mobile Content Management — securing corporate files and documents on devices), and **IAM** (Identity and Access Management — controlling who can authenticate to what) into one unified platform. Examples the transcript names: Microsoft Intune, VMware Workspace ONE, Jamf Pro.

---

### Application containerization

**Application containerization** creates an encrypted, isolated container on the device that holds corporate apps and data, completely separated from personal apps. Examples: Samsung Knox, Citrix Secure Hub.

- Corporate data inside the container is encrypted and invisible to personal apps
- Clipboard and data-sharing between container and personal apps is blocked by policy — stopping leakage through actions as simple as copy-paste
- Admins can wipe the container without affecting personal content

> Think of the container like a locked briefcase inside a backpack — your personal stuff is in the backpack, but corporate files are sealed inside a case only the employer can open or destroy.

### Sideloading and application controls

**Sideloading** is installing apps from sources outside the official app store (App Store or Google Play). Sideloaded apps can contain malware targeting corporate data or credentials.

- **iOS supervised mode**: MDM can completely block non-App Store installs
- **Android**: MDM policy disables the "unknown sources" setting that enables sideloading
- Organizations can host internal apps in **enterprise app stores** pushed via MDM, enabling private distribution without going through public stores

---

### Remote access controls

- **Per-app VPN** — routes only specific corporate app traffic through the VPN; personal app traffic goes directly to the internet. Balances protection and performance.
- **Always-on VPN** — all device traffic passes through the corporate VPN at all times; highest inspection and control level
- MDM pushes VPN configurations so users don't set them up manually

---

### Geofencing

**Geofencing** uses GPS or Wi-Fi positioning to define a geographic boundary and automatically trigger policy changes or alerts when a device crosses it.

- Restrict corporate resource access when a device is detected outside approved areas
- Automatically disable camera when a device enters a sensitive facility; re-enable when it leaves
- Alert when a device exits a defined perimeter

> Geofencing is like an invisible electric fence for device policy — cross the boundary and the rules change automatically.

### Mobile security threats

| Threat | What It Is | Key Control |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| **Sideloading** | Malicious apps from outside official stores | MDM policy blocking unknown sources |
| **Smishing** | Phishing delivered via SMS | User awareness training |
| **Wi-Fi attacks** | Traffic interception on open/poorly secured networks | VPN enforcement |
| **Physical theft** | Device lost or stolen | Screen lock, encryption, remote wipe |
| **Bluetooth attacks** | Short-range wireless exploitation | Disable auto-pairing; keep Bluetooth off when not in use |

### Certificate management via MDM

When an organization runs an internal **PKI** (Public Key Infrastructure — the system of certificate authorities and digital certificates used to verify identity and enable encrypted communication), MDM can push the organization's **root CA certificate** (root Certificate Authority certificate — the top-level certificate that establishes trust for all certificates the internal CA issues) to every enrolled device's trusted certificate store.

This means:
- Internal TLS certificates are trusted automatically — no manual install per user
- VPN client certs, Wi-Fi **EAP-TLS** (Extensible Authentication Protocol-TLS — a method for authenticating to Wi-Fi using certificates instead of passwords) certs, and email signing/encryption certs are all delivered via MDM

### Wireless site surveys

A **wireless site survey** is the process of physically moving through a facility with measurement tools to assess signal coverage, find interference, and map access point placement.

- **Heat maps** — visual representations of signal strength across physical space; show dead zones AND where signal leaks outside the building
- **Spectrum analyzers** — tools that detect all signals in a frequency band, including non-802.11 sources like microwave ovens, Bluetooth devices, and neighboring networks that cause interference
- Surveys should be repeated when construction, floor plans, or neighboring networks change
- Reducing signal leakage outside the facility shrinks the area from which an attacker could attempt to connect or eavesdrop

> A site survey is like a fire marshal walking the building with a smoke detector — you're finding gaps in coverage *before* something goes wrong.

---

### Recap

- **BYOD** = lowest cost, lowest IT control; **COBO** = most restrictive; **COPE/CYOD** = company-owned middle ground
- **MDM** manages whole devices; **MAM** manages specific apps; **EMM** combines both with content and identity management
- **Containerization** isolates corporate data in an encrypted space and blocks clipboard leakage between corporate and personal apps
- **Geofencing** triggers automatic policy changes based on physical location (e.g., disabling camera in a secure facility)
- **Wireless site surveys** plus heat maps identify coverage gaps and signal leakage; spectrum analyzers catch non-Wi-Fi interference

### Exam flags

- **BYOD vs. COPE vs. COBO**: know which model offers which level of IT control and what remote wipe capabilities each allows
- **MDM vs. MAM**: MDM controls the entire device; MAM controls only specific apps — MAM is the preferred approach when employees resist full MDM enrollment (BYOD scenarios)
- **Geofencing triggers**: know that it uses GPS/Wi-Fi positioning to *automatically* enforce policy changes, not just send alerts
