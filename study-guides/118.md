## Audits and Assessments

This lesson covers how organizations formally evaluate their security programs — through audits, assessments, and regulatory examinations — and why those evaluations matter for compliance, credibility, and continuous improvement.

---

### Internal vs. external audits

An **internal audit** is a formal security or compliance review conducted by the organization's own audit or security team.

- Scheduled flexibly; scoped to specific concerns
- Catches gaps *before* external auditors do, enabling quiet remediation
- Key weakness: **lack of independence** — the same people who built the controls may be assessing them
- Fix: have the audit function report to the **audit committee** — a board-level governance body that sits above the CIO/CISO — creating separation between implementers and assessors

An **external audit** is conducted by an independent third party with no prior relationship to the organization.

- Required for formal certifications: **ISO 27001** (international information security management standard), **SOC 2** (service organization security controls report issued by a CPA firm), **PCI DSS** (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard), **HIPAA** (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)
- Higher stakeholder credibility because the auditor has no political stake in the outcome
- More expensive and requires coordination: scope definition, evidence access, interviews

> Think of an internal audit like grading your own homework — useful for practice, but your teacher (the external auditor) still has to sign off before the grade counts.

---

### Types of audits

| Audit type | What it checks |
|---|---|
| **Compliance audit** | Whether the org meets a specific regulation or standard (PCI DSS, HIPAA, SOX) |
| **SOX IT controls audit** | IT controls affecting *financial reporting* — are they designed and operating correctly? (**SOX** = Sarbanes-Oxley Act, a law requiring public companies to attest to financial control accuracy) |
| **Security control audit** | Effectiveness of security controls against a framework such as **NIST 800-53** or ISO 27001 |
| **Financial audit** | Accuracy of financial statements; conducted by a **CPA** — Certified Public Accountant — firm |
| **Operational audit** | Efficiency and effectiveness of business and security processes |

---

### Attestation

**Attestation** is a formal, legally accountable statement by a qualified auditor or executive affirming that specific conditions are true.

- A **SOC 2 Type 2** attestation: a CPA firm affirms that a service org's controls were well-designed *and* operated effectively over a defined time period
- **Management attestation** (required by SOX): senior executives personally declare responsibility for the effectiveness of internal controls — making them *personally liable* if the attestation is false

> Signing an attestation is like signing a legal affidavit: you're not just saying "things look fine" — you're on the hook if they aren't.

---

### Assessment types

Assessments are less formal than audits but still structured evaluations. Unlike audits, they vary widely in scope and methodology.

| Assessment | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Vulnerability assessment** | Scans systems for known weaknesses using automated tools + manual analysis; assigns severity using **CVSS** (Common Vulnerability Scoring System — a 0–10 numeric scale); does *not* exploit vulnerabilities |
| **Risk assessment** | Identifies threats and vulnerabilities, analyzes likelihood and impact, produces a prioritized risk picture |
| **Security assessment** | Evaluates overall security posture via documents, interviews, observation, and technical testing |
| **Threat assessment** | Evaluates specific threats the org faces based on threat intelligence and industry data |
| **Gap assessment** | Compares current controls against a target framework to identify what's missing |

---

### Regulatory examinations

**Regulatory examinations** are government-compelled reviews that differ from voluntary audits: the regulator has legal authority to demand access and impose sanctions.

- **Banking:** FDIC, OCC, Federal Reserve, state regulators
- **Securities:** SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission)
- **Healthcare:** OCR within HHS (Office for Civil Rights, Department of Health and Human Services)
- Unlike third-party audits, you cannot opt out or limit scope

### The audit process

**Planning → Evidence Collection → Analysis → Reporting → Follow-up**

Evidence collection techniques:

- **Documentation review** — policies, procedures, diagrams, config baselines
- **Inquiry/interviews** — how controls actually work in practice
- **Observation** — watching processes happen in real time
- **Inspection** — examining physical assets, logs, config files, ACLs
- **Re-performance** — auditor independently runs the control to verify it works
- **Analytical procedures** — comparing metrics and trends to find anomalies

---

### Continuous monitoring and bug bounties

**Continuous monitoring** shifts from point-in-time snapshots to ongoing real-time assessment.

- Automated compliance scanners alert immediately on noncompliant configs
- **SIEM** — Security Information and Event Management platform — collects and correlates security events continuously
- Dashboards track **KPIs** — Key Performance Indicators — between formal audits
- Produces a running evidence body instead of a last-minute scramble before audit day

**Bug bounty programs** extend this with crowd-sourced human testing: independent researchers find and responsibly disclose vulnerabilities in exchange for monetary rewards. They catch what automated scanners miss because humans apply creative analysis, not just signature matching.

---

### Recap

- Internal audits are flexible but risk bias; external audits are credible but costly and required for formal certifications
- Attestation (SOC 2 Type 2, SOX management attestation) creates *personal legal accountability* for what's signed
- Assessments (vulnerability, risk, gap, threat) differ from audits in scope and formality — vulnerability assessments identify but do **not** exploit
- Regulatory examinations are compelled by law; regulators can demand access and impose sanctions
- Continuous monitoring + bug bounties fill the gaps between periodic formal audits

### Exam flags

- **Internal vs. external audit** — know the independence limitation of internal audits and which certifications *require* external audits (ISO 27001, SOC 2, PCI DSS, HIPAA)
- **Attestation** — know what SOC 2 Type 2 and SOX management attestation are and why they create legal accountability
- **Assessment types** — be able to distinguish vulnerability (scan/categorize, no exploitation) vs. risk vs. gap vs. threat assessments
