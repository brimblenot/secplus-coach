## Physical Attacks (Domain 2)

Physical attacks bypass every software and network defense by targeting hardware directly. If an attacker can touch the machine, most digital controls become irrelevant — and the Security+ exam tests whether you know exactly how each physical attack works and how to stop it.

---

### Brute force physical entry

**Brute force** (physical context) means using raw force — breaking locks, cutting fences, ramming barriers — to reach hardware inside a facility.

- Once inside, an attacker can remove drives, install implants, or reset credentials via out-of-band management
- Defense-in-depth here means **layers**: detection + delay + response, not one strong lock
- **Tailgating/piggybacking** — following an authorized person through a controlled door without badging in — exploits social politeness rather than technical weakness
- A **mantrap vestibule** — a double-door airlock that admits only one person at a time — physically prevents tailgating regardless of social pressure

> *Think of a mantrap like a revolving door that jams if two people try to share a section — social dynamics don't override the mechanism.*

---

### Card skimming

**Card skimming** is the attachment of hidden hardware to legitimate card readers to silently steal card data and PINs during normal transactions.

- **ATM skimmer overlay** — a fake fascia fitted over the card slot that reads the magnetic stripe as the card enters
- Hidden camera or **false PIN pad overlay** captures the PIN simultaneously
- **Gas pump skimmers** are installed inside the pump housing; often use **Bluetooth** — a short-range wireless protocol — to send stolen data to a nearby attacker
- **Shimmer device** — a razor-thin wafer inserted *inside* the chip card slot that reads chip data; enables **card-not-present fraud** (online purchases where no physical chip is needed)
- Defenses: inspect readers before use, cover the PIN pad, use contactless payment

> *A skimmer is like a photocopier secretly taped inside a mailbox slot — every letter that passes through gets duplicated.*

---

### RFID cloning

**RFID** — Radio Frequency Identification — is the wireless technology in building access badges, key fobs, and some payment cards. **RFID cloning** captures a card's transmitted identifier and writes it to a blank card.

- HID proximity cards broadcast a static ID when energized — readable through a wallet in seconds with cheap hardware (<$50)
- Cloned card grants access to every area the original authorized
- Contactless payment cards generate a **dynamic cryptogram** — a one-time code — per transaction, so a static clone cannot complete a payment; older static-ID cards lack this protection
- Defenses: **RFID-blocking wallet** (attenuates the radio field), multi-factor physical access (card + PIN or biometric), and monitoring access logs for the same card appearing in two places simultaneously

> *Cloning an RFID badge is like recording someone's voice saying "open sesame" and playing it back at the door — works perfectly until the door requires a fingerprint too.*

---

### USB-based attacks

Operating systems trust USB devices implicitly — no authentication required. Attackers exploit this.

| Device | What it is | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **USB Rubber Ducky** | Thumb-drive form-factor keystroke injector by Hak5 | Emulates a USB keyboard; fires pre-programmed keystrokes at machine speed (hundreds of chars/sec) |
| **O.MG Cable** | Visually identical USB charging cable with embedded wireless microcomputer | Acts as a remotely controlled keyboard over Wi-Fi while appearing to just charge a phone |
| **USBkill** | Capacitor-based USB device | Charges from host power, discharges high voltage back through data lines — destroys the USB controller and potentially the motherboard |

- These attacks **bypass antivirus** (no file written to disk) and **bypass application allowlisting** — a security policy that permits only approved programs to run — because they invoke legitimate OS utilities like PowerShell by *typing* commands
- **Baiting** — leaving enticing USB drives in parking lots or mailing them to targets — exploits curiosity; studies show 45–98% of found drives get plugged in
- Defenses: USB port restriction via endpoint software, **device allowlisting** for authorized peripherals, physical **USB port blockers** (hardware plugs requiring a key to remove)

> *A Rubber Ducky is a burglar who doesn't pick the lock — it just walks up to the keyboard and types the door open itself.*

---

### Juice jacking

**Juice jacking** is the compromise of public USB charging stations (airports, hotels) to steal data or install malware on connected devices while charging them.

- Defenses: **power-only USB cable** (data lines physically absent), AC adapter instead of USB port, or a **USB data blocker** — a small pass-through dongle that breaks data lines while passing power

> *A compromised charging station is like a friendly stranger offering to charge your phone — then reading every text while they do it.*

---

### Cold boot attacks

**DRAM** — Dynamic Random-Access Memory — retains its contents for seconds to minutes after power is cut, especially when cooled rapidly. A **cold boot attack** exploits this to recover encryption keys or session tokens from RAM after a system is powered off.

- Full disk encryption protects stored data but not keys already loaded into RAM
- Defenses: CPU-level memory encryption, secure memory overwrite before shutdown, physical controls preventing access to running systems

---

### Hardware implants

**Hardware implants** are physical devices secretly installed inside equipment to provide persistent access or data capture below the OS level.

- Examples from the **NSA ANT catalog** (leaked 2013): **COTTONMOUTH** (USB implant), **IRONCHEF** (PCI slot backdoor), **RAGEMASTER** (VGA cable tap for video monitoring)
- Simple version: a **hardware keylogger** plugged between keyboard and computer
- Defenses: tamper-evident chassis seals, hardware chain of custody from trusted suppliers, physical inspection

---

### Environmental attacks

**Environmental attacks** target the infrastructure computing equipment depends on, not the equipment itself.

- Power: cutting external electrical panels or draining generator fuel causes facility-wide outage
- **HVAC** — Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning — compromise causes overheating and thermal shutdown
- Fire suppression activation displaces oxygen and disables equipment
- Flooding from overhead plumbing or external water intrusion

---

### Defensive controls summary

| Control | What it stops |
|---|---|
| Mantrap vestibule | Tailgating |
| RFID-blocking wallet | Passive RFID cloning |
| USB port blocker | USB attacks on unused ports |
| USB data blocker | Juice jacking |
| Tamper-evident seals | Implant installation |
| **Degaussing** (magnetic erasure) / physical destruction per **NIST SP 800-88** | Data recovery from discarded media |
| **BIOS/UEFI password** — firmware-level boot password | Unauthorized boot from external media |
| Full disk encryption | Data recovery from stolen drives |
| **Faraday cage** — RF-shielded room or enclosure | Wireless exfiltration and remote RF attacks |

---

### Recap

- Physical access defeats most digital controls — drive removal, implants, cold boot, and credential resets all become possible
- Card skimmers steal magnetic stripe data; **shimmers** steal chip data for online fraud; both are defeated by covering PINs and using contactless payment
- USB Rubber Ducky and O.MG Cable bypass AV and allowlisting by typing commands as a trusted keyboard — no file ever touches disk
- RFID cloning is passive, fast, and cheap — multi-factor physical access (card + PIN/biometric) is the key mitigation
- Cold boot attacks recover RAM contents after power-off; full disk encryption alone does not stop this

### Exam flags

- **USB HID attack mechanics** — why Rubber Ducky/O.MG Cable bypass antivirus and allowlisting (keyboard emulation, no file written)
- **Shimmer vs. skimmer** — what each captures and which fraud type each enables (card-present cloning vs. card-not-present online use)
- **Cold boot attack** — what it exploits (DRAM data remanence), why full disk encryption is insufficient, and what mitigates it
