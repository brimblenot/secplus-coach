## User Training (Domain 5)

User training is the targeted, role-specific instruction that teaches employees *how* to act securely in their actual jobs — not just why security matters. This lesson covers who needs what training, and how to deliver it so it actually sticks.

---

### Training vs. awareness: not the same thing

**Security awareness** is the organization-wide effort to build a security-conscious culture — phishing simulations, posters, broad policy reminders. It creates the *mindset* to care.

**User training** delivers the *knowledge and skills* to act on that mindset correctly, targeted to a specific role's actual risks.

- Awareness = motivation; training = capability
- Both are necessary; neither alone is sufficient

> Think of awareness as the "buckle up" campaign and training as the actual driver's ed class.

---

### Role-based training: who needs what

A one-size-fits-all program wastes time on irrelevant risks while under-training on real ones. Effective programs start by mapping each role's data access, decisions, and likely attack vectors.

**Finance/accounting staff** — highest BEC risk
- **BEC (Business Email Compromise)** — a social engineering attack where attackers impersonate executives or vendors to trick finance employees into wiring money or changing vendor payment details
- Train on: callback verification (call the requestor back using an *internal directory* number, never the email's number), dual authorization for large transactions, recognizing artificial urgency and secrecy as red flags

**IT staff/system administrators** — highest privilege risk
- A compromised admin account lets an attacker move laterally, escalate privileges, and cover tracks organization-wide
- Train on: **least privilege** (give accounts only the access they need), **jump servers/privileged access workstations** — dedicated hardened machines used only for sensitive admin tasks, separate accounts for admin vs. daily use, audit logging, change management procedures, incident response evidence preservation

**Developers** — create vulnerabilities other controls must defend against
- **OWASP Top Ten** — a prioritized list of the most prevalent web application vulnerability categories (injection, broken authentication, XSS, broken access control, etc.)
- **Input validation** — rejecting malformed or malicious data before it reaches application logic
- **Output encoding** — neutralizing untrusted data before rendering it in HTML to prevent XSS
- **Parameterized queries** — a coding technique that prevents SQL injection by separating query structure from user-supplied data
- **Secrets management** — never hard-coding credentials or API keys in source code; instead using dedicated vaults (**HashiCorp Vault**, **AWS Secrets Manager**, **Azure Key Vault** — tools that store and serve secrets securely at runtime)
- **SBOM (Software Bill of Materials)** — an inventory of all third-party libraries and components in an application, used to track known-vulnerable dependencies

**HR/people managers** — handle sensitive personnel data and control access provisioning
- Train on: **GDPR** (EU data privacy regulation) and applicable state privacy laws governing employee data collection, retention, and deletion
- Onboarding: collect signed security acknowledgments, verify training completion before granting access
- **Offboarding**: for involuntary terminations, revoke *all* access at or before the moment the employee is informed — a disgruntled employee with active access is an immediate insider threat
- Recognize insider threat behavioral indicators: unusual data access, expressions of grievance, unexplained financial stress, access requests beyond role

**Executives/board members** — high-value, highly targeted
- **Whaling** — phishing attacks crafted specifically for executive targets, using personalized details from LinkedIn, public speeches, and filings to build convincing pretexts
- **Deepfake technology** — synthetic audio or video of an executive that can be used to authorize fraudulent transactions
- Train on: personal digital footprint awareness, secure transmission of board materials, verifying unexpected requests through independent channels, governance responsibilities (setting security culture tone, understanding CISO risk reports)

**All users — general training baseline**
- **MFA (Multi-Factor Authentication)** — requiring a second proof of identity beyond a password, substantially harder to compromise than passwords alone
- Password managers, unique passwords per account, no reuse
- Phishing recognition: hover links before clicking, check sender domains, watch for urgency/authority pressure
- **Juice jacking** — a risk where public USB charging stations are rigged to transfer malware to connected devices; use AC adapters or data-blocking cables instead
- **MDM (Mobile Device Management)** — software organizations install on work devices to enforce security policies; employees should understand what it does and why
- **Side-loading** — installing apps from unofficial sources outside the official app store, which bypasses security vetting
- Public Wi-Fi: access org data only over **VPN** (an encrypted tunnel); unencrypted public Wi-Fi exposes traffic to interception
- Incident reporting: every employee must know *what* to report, *how*, and *to whom* — early reporting is the difference between rapid containment and extended compromise

### Training delivery: how it works matters as much as what's taught

| Approach | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Microlearning** (5–10 min modules spread across the year) | Better retention than a single long annual session |
| **Scenario-based learning** | Trainees practice real decisions, not abstract concepts |
| **Pre-testing** | Identifies actual knowledge gaps; avoids wasting time on what's already known |
| **Post-testing** | Measures knowledge gain |
| **Phishing simulations** | Measures whether knowledge translates to *behavior* change — the real goal |
| **Spaced repetition** | Brief reinforcement over time produces more durable retention than one concentrated event |
| **LMS (Learning Management System)** | Tracks completion and compliance across the organization |

> Think of phishing simulations like fire drills: you're not testing knowledge of fire safety theory — you're testing whether people actually go out the door.

---

### Recap

- Training builds role-specific *skills*; awareness builds organization-wide *mindset* — both are required
- Finance staff: train on BEC, callback verification, dual authorization; IT staff: least privilege, jump servers, change management; developers: OWASP Top Ten, secrets management, parameterized queries; HR: offboarding access revocation is time-critical; executives: whaling and deepfake verification
- All users need MFA, phishing recognition, USB/juice jacking awareness, public Wi-Fi + VPN, and incident reporting procedures
- Delivery method matters: microlearning + scenario-based + spaced repetition + phishing simulations outperform annual lecture-style modules
- Phishing simulation results are the behavioral measure of training effectiveness, not just quiz scores

---

### Exam flags

- **BEC scenario questions** — expect a finance-staff scenario and be ready to identify callback verification and dual authorization as the correct controls
- **Whaling vs. phishing** — know that whaling is phishing *specifically targeting executives*, using personalized research to craft the pretext
- **Offboarding timing** — involuntary termination access revocation must happen *at or before* the moment the employee is informed
