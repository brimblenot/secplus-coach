## Phishing (Domain 2)

Phishing is the most common initial-access technique attackers use against organizations, and it spans far more than just email. This lesson covers every major variant, the technical tricks that make them convincing, and the defenses that stop them.

---

### What phishing is and how it works

**Phishing** is a social-engineering attack delivered through electronic communications — usually email — where the attacker impersonates a trusted entity to trick the target into revealing credentials, sensitive data, or performing an action (like wiring money).

- Relies on **urgency, fear, and authority** to override critical thinking ("Your account is compromised — act now!")
- Impersonates banks, IT helpdesks, shipping carriers, government agencies, or popular web services
- Generic phishing: sent to millions with no personalization; low success rate is offset by sheer volume

*Think of generic phishing like junk mail blasted to every address on the street — most people toss it, but enough bite to make it worth it.*

---

### Targeted variants

**Spear phishing** is a personalized phishing attack that uses real details about the target — their name, job title, colleagues, current projects, or vendors — gathered from LinkedIn, company sites, social media, and prior data breaches — to craft a believable, individualized message.

- Dramatically higher success rate than generic phishing
- Primary initial-access method for **APT groups** — Advanced Persistent Threat actors, i.e., sophisticated, long-term attackers — and organized cybercriminals

**Whaling** is spear phishing aimed specifically at **C-suite executives** (CEO, CFO, COO, General Counsel) because they can authorize large wire transfers, access sensitive systems, and override normal procedures.

- Messages are polished and professionally written
- Goals: fraudulent wire transfers, executive email/cloud credential theft

**Vishing** (**voice phishing**) uses telephone calls instead of email. Attackers impersonate bank fraud teams, IRS agents, or IT helpdesk staff and manufacture urgency to extract credit card numbers, SSNs, or remote-access credentials.

- **Caller ID spoofing** via **VoIP** — Voice over Internet Protocol, i.e., phone calls carried over the internet — makes calls appear to come from a bank's real number
- **Defense:** never give sensitive info to inbound callers; hang up and call back using a number from the official source (back of your card, official website)

**Smishing** is phishing via **SMS** (text message), typically posing as shipping carriers (USPS, FedEx) or banks.

- Smartphone screens truncate URLs, making destination inspection hard
- Casual texting pace reduces scrutiny; mobile browsers show less security info

**Quishing** is phishing using malicious **QR codes** — quick-response barcodes that encode a URL — printed as stickers placed over legitimate codes (restaurant menus, parking meters) or embedded in emails.

- Users cannot read the destination URL before scanning — they're already loading the page before they realize the risk
- QR codes in email often bypass link-scanning filters that don't decode them

*A quishing sticker on a parking meter is like a pickpocket swapping the real price tag for their own before you even look at the item.*

### Business Email Compromise

**BEC** — **Business Email Compromise** — is a fraud attack where the attacker impersonates an executive or vendor to authorize fraudulent financial transactions, typically wire transfers or payment-detail changes.

- **No malware involved** — pure social engineering
- Attacker either spoofs the executive's address or fully compromises their real account
- Causes billions in annual losses; one of the most financially destructive attack categories

### Technical tricks that make phishing convincing

| Technique | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Domain spoofing** | Forges the email From header, or registers a lookalike domain (amazon-security.com vs. amazon.com) |
| **Typosquatting** | Registers common misspellings of a domain (goggle.com, amazoon.com) |
| **Homograph attack** | Substitutes visually identical Unicode characters (Cyrillic "а" for Latin "a") so "apple.com" looks correct but resolves to a different domain |
| **URL obfuscation** | Uses URL shorteners, redirect chains, or deep subdirectory paths to hide the real destination |
| **HTML link trick** | Link text displays a legitimate-looking URL; the actual `href` points to the attacker's site — hovering reveals the true destination |
| **Prepending** | Adds "SAFE:" or "Re:" to subject lines or visible URL fragments to suggest prior trust or scanning |

**Pharming** is distinct from phishing: instead of tricking users into clicking a bad link, it corrupts **DNS resolution** — the system that translates domain names to IP addresses — so users who type the *correct* URL are silently redirected to an attacker-controlled site. Accomplished via **DNS cache poisoning** (corrupting a resolver's cached records) or modifying the **hosts file** (a local file on a computer that maps domain names to IPs before DNS is consulted) on the victim's machine.

*Pharming is like secretly updating every street sign in town — even careful drivers following the rules end up at the wrong destination.*

---

### Defenses

| Defense | What it does |
|---|---|
| **SPF** (Sender Policy Framework) | DNS record listing authorized mail servers for a domain; receivers reject mail from others |
| **DKIM** (DomainKeys Identified Mail) | Adds a cryptographic signature to email headers; receiver validates it against the sender's DNS public key |
| **DMARC** (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance) | Ties SPF + DKIM together; policy tells receivers to **none** (monitor), **quarantine**, or **reject** failing messages; enables spoofing reports |
| **MFA** (Multi-Factor Authentication) | Even if a password is stolen via phishing, the attacker can't log in without the second factor |
| **Email gateway filtering** | Uses threat intel, reputation data, and **sandboxing** (running attachments in an isolated environment) before delivery |
| **Simulated phishing + training** | Org sends fake phishing emails to employees; failures trigger targeted training; measurably lowers click rates |

*SPF/DKIM/DMARC work together like a postal authentication system: SPF checks who's allowed to send, DKIM seals the envelope, and DMARC tells the post office what to do when the seal is broken.*

---

### Recap

- **Spear phishing** uses personal research for high success; **whaling** targets executives specifically
- **Vishing/smishing/quishing** extend phishing beyond email to calls, texts, and QR codes
- **BEC** is pure social engineering — no malware — focused on fraudulent financial transactions
- **Homograph attacks** exploit Unicode look-alikes; **pharming** attacks DNS, not user clicks
- **SPF + DKIM + DMARC** are the layered email authentication stack; **MFA** is the critical compensating control if credentials are stolen

### Exam flags

- **Scenario-based variant ID:** given a description (targeted executive, no malware, wire transfer) → identify BEC vs. whaling vs. spear phishing
- **SPF / DKIM / DMARC roles:** know what each one specifically does and how they relate to each other
- **Pharming vs. phishing distinction:** pharming attacks DNS infrastructure; phishing attacks the user through deceptive messages
