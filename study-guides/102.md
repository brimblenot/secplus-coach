## Incident Planning

Incident planning is everything you do *before* a security event hits — writing the plan, training the team, rehearsing procedures, and locking in legal relationships — so that when something goes wrong, the response is fast, consistent, and legally compliant. Poor planning means improvised response; improvised response means more damage.

---

### The Incident Response Plan (IRP)

The **IRP** — Incident Response Plan — is the master document that defines exactly how the organization handles security incidents. It must exist before any incident occurs and be reviewed at least annually (or after major infrastructure changes, regulatory shifts, or real incidents).

A complete IRP contains these sections:

- **Policy statement** — management's commitment and the authority granted to the response team (e.g., permission to isolate systems, preserve evidence)
- **Purpose & scope** — what events and systems are covered; how this plan relates to the BCP (Business Continuity Plan) and DRP (Disaster Recovery Plan)
- **Roles & responsibilities** — CSIRT membership, contact info, alternates, escalation paths
- **Definitions** — what counts as an "incident," severity levels, and thresholds for each
- **IR procedures** — step-by-step guidance per incident type
- **Communication plan** — who gets notified, at what severity, through what channel, including external parties (legal, regulators, law enforcement, customers)
- **Evidence handling** — collection, preservation, and legal compliance procedures
- **Training & testing requirements** — how often, what exercises, and how results feed back into the plan

> Think of the IRP like an airline's emergency procedures manual: pilots don't invent the evacuation sequence mid-flight — they follow a pre-written, pre-rehearsed plan so that panic doesn't drive decisions.

### CSIRT structure options

The **CSIRT** — Computer Security Incident Response Team — is the group responsible for executing the IRP. Organizations structure it in three main ways:

| Model | Who's on it | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dedicated** | Full-time IR professionals | Fastest, most practiced | Expensive; only viable for large orgs |
| **Virtual** | Employees from IT, legal, HR, comms, ops with IR as a secondary role | Cost-effective | Slower to mobilize, less practiced |
| **Hybrid** | Small dedicated core + on-call specialists + external DFIR vendor | Balances cost and capability | Requires coordination across groups |

**DFIR** — Digital Forensics and Incident Response — vendors (e.g., Mandiant, CrowdStrike Services, Secureworks) provide specialist capacity for major incidents.

### Third-party retainer agreements

A **retainer agreement** is a pre-negotiated contract with an external IR firm, signed *before* any incident occurs.

- The firm already knows your environment and team
- Legal/commercial terms are pre-agreed, so they can start immediately
- The agreement defines: response-time **SLA** (Service Level Agreement — a contractual commitment to respond within a set timeframe), scope of services, hourly rates, and escalation procedures

> Trying to hire an IR firm *during* an active breach is like calling a plumber to negotiate a contract while your basement is flooding — every minute spent on paperwork is more water damage.

### Playbooks vs. runbooks

These two terms are often confused. They serve different levels of decision-making:

| Document | Level | Contains |
|---|---|---|
| **Playbook** | Strategic | Decision trees: when to escalate, when to contain, when to notify leadership or regulators |
| **Runbook** | Tactical/technical | Exact commands and tools for specific tasks (e.g., memory capture with WinPmem, disk imaging with dcfldd including hash verification) |

**WinPmem** — a tool for capturing a live memory image from a Windows or Linux system. **dcfldd** — a forensic disk imaging tool (an enhanced version of `dd`) that computes cryptographic hashes during imaging to verify integrity.

Runbooks eliminate the need to recall technical details under stress, reducing errors and ensuring every responder follows the same procedure.

### Legal and regulatory obligations

Knowing your notification deadlines *before* an incident means you execute, not research, when the clock is running.

| Regulation | Deadline | Notify whom |
|---|---|---|
| **GDPR** (EU data privacy law) | 72 hours of discovery | Supervisory authority |
| **HIPAA** (US healthcare data law) | 60 days of discovery | Affected individuals + HHS; media if >500 in a state |
| **State breach laws** | Varies | Affected residents; varies by state |

**HHS** — the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, which enforces HIPAA.

**Legal hold** procedures must be pre-established: when litigation is anticipated, relevant data must be preserved *immediately* before normal deletion processes destroy it.

Pre-established relationships with **FBI Cyber Division**, **CISA** (Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency — the federal civilian cyber defense agency), and sector-specific information-sharing organizations also eliminate delays when engagement is needed.

### Communication during an incident

- Pre-drafted templates (breach notification letters, media statements, regulatory filings) allow legal review *before* a crisis
- Pre-approved contact lists for CEO, General Counsel, CISO, board, PR lead, and investor relations must be maintained and tested
- **Need-to-know restriction**: broadcasting that a breach is active can tip off an insider or alert an attacker still in the environment to pivot or accelerate — information stays compartmentalized until the attacker is fully ejected

### Training and exercise types

| Exercise type | What happens | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Annual IR training** | All CSIRT members review the current plan and their roles | Yearly |
| **Tabletop exercise** | Discussion-based walkthrough of a scenario; no real systems activated; injects added mid-scenario to complicate the response | Quarterly+ |
| **Functional exercise** | Tests one specific component in isolation (e.g., backup restoration, notification tree timing) | As needed |
| **Full-scale exercise** | Activates actual IR procedures against test systems end-to-end | Periodically |

**Scenario injects** — mid-exercise complications that force teams to adapt (e.g., "the backup system is also compromised").

Post-exercise reviews produce action items that feed directly back into plan improvements — this is the continuous improvement cycle.

### Framework references

**NIST SP 800-61** — NIST Special Publication 800-61, the *Computer Security Incident Handling Guide* — is the authoritative incident planning framework reference for the Security+ exam.

The **MITRE ATT&CK framework** — a structured taxonomy (classification system) of adversary **TTPs** (Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures — the specific methods attackers use) — complements planning by helping teams build realistic exercise scenarios, ensure playbooks cover the full range of attacker behavior, and identify detection gaps by mapping existing controls against the ATT&CK matrix.

---

### Recap

- The **IRP** must exist before incidents, be reviewed annually, and contain eight defined components including a communication plan and evidence handling procedures
- **CSIRT** structure choices (dedicated, virtual, hybrid) trade cost against response speed; retainer agreements with DFIR vendors must be pre-signed, not negotiated mid-incident
- **Playbooks** guide strategic decisions; **runbooks** contain exact technical commands — both eliminate guesswork under pressure
- **GDPR** = 72-hour notification; **HIPAA** = 60-day notification; know deadlines before the clock starts
- **NIST SP 800-61** is the exam's authoritative IR framework; **MITRE ATT&CK** maps adversary TTPs to improve planning and gap analysis

### Exam flags

- **NIST SP 800-61** as the named framework reference — the exam will test that you can identify this by name and purpose
- **Playbook vs. runbook distinction** — strategic decision tree vs. specific technical commands; confusing them is a common wrong-answer trap
- **GDPR 72-hour and HIPAA 60-day notification deadlines** — specific numbers are high-probability exam targets
