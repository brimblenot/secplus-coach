## Security Standards

Security standards translate high-level policy intent into specific, measurable, auditable requirements. This lesson covers the major frameworks and regulations you'll encounter on the exam — what each one is, who it applies to, and what makes it distinct.

---

### What a standard actually is

A **security standard** is a prescriptive document that specifies *exactly* how security must be implemented — which algorithms, which configurations, which controls — as opposed to a policy, which states only the general intent (e.g., "all data must be protected").

- Standards are **technology-specific and auditable**; policies are not
- Organizations derive standards from their own risk analysis or from established external frameworks

> Think of a policy as "keep the building secure" and the standard as the blueprint specifying lock grades, alarm models, and key-rotation schedules.

---

### NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)

The **NIST CSF** — **N**ational **I**nstitute of **S**tandards and **T**echnology **C**ybersecurity **F**ramework — is a voluntary risk-management framework organized around five core functions:

| Function | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Identify** | Asset management, governance, risk assessment |
| **Protect** | Access control, training, data security, maintenance |
| **Detect** | Continuous monitoring, anomaly detection |
| **Respond** | Response planning, analysis, mitigation |
| **Recover** | Restoration of capabilities, communications |

The CSF also defines **four implementation tiers**: Tier 1 (**Partial** — informal, ad hoc practices) through Tier 4 (**Adaptive** — continuous improvement using threat intelligence). Organizations use **framework profiles** to align cybersecurity activities with their specific business requirements and risk tolerance.

> Think of the five functions as the lifecycle of a hospital emergency department: assess incoming patients (Identify), put on gloves (Protect), monitor vitals (Detect), respond to a crash (Respond), discharge and follow up (Recover).

### NIST SP 800-53

**NIST SP 800-53** — *Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations* — is a comprehensive control catalog originally built for U.S. federal agencies but widely adopted in the private sector. Controls are grouped into families identified by two-letter prefixes (e.g., **AC** = Access Control, **AU** = Audit and Accountability, **IR** = Incident Response, **IA** = Identification and Authentication).

- Defines three **impact baselines**: Low, Moderate, and High
- Higher-impact systems require more extensive controls

---

### NIST SP 800-171 and CMMC

**NIST SP 800-171** — *Protecting Controlled Unclassified Information in Nonfederal Systems* — defines **110 security requirements** across 14 families for contractors handling **CUI** (**C**ontrolled **U**nclassified **I**nformation) on behalf of the federal government.

**CMMC** — **C**ybersecurity **M**aturity **M**odel **C**ertification — is the **Department of Defense** framework that enforces these requirements across the defense industrial base:

| Level | Name | Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundational | 17 basic cyber hygiene practices |
| 2 | Advanced | All 110 NIST SP 800-171 requirements |
| 3 | Expert | 800-171 + 24 additional practices |

- Levels 2 and 3 require third-party assessments by **C3PAOs** (**CMMC Third Party Assessment Organizations**) — not self-attestation

> If you built a full-stack app and a DoD contractor wanted to host it, they'd need at minimum Level 2 CMMC certification before you'd hand over any CUI.

### ISO 27001 and ISO 27002

**ISO 27001** is the international standard for an **ISMS** (**I**nformation **S**ecurity **M**anagement **S**ystem) — the structured program an organization builds to identify risks and implement proportionate controls. Certification is granted after independent audit and signals to customers and regulators that security is formally managed.

**ISO 27002** is the companion best-practice guide providing detailed implementation guidance for the controls referenced in ISO 27001. Together they cover 14 domains including access control, cryptography, physical security, and incident management.

> ISO 27001 is like earning a restaurant health inspection certification: an outside auditor verifies your kitchen meets the standard, not just that you said you'd keep it clean.

### SOC 2

**SOC 2** — **S**ervice **O**rganization **C**ontrol 2, developed by the **AICPA** (**A**merican **I**nstitute of **C**ertified **P**ublic **A**ccountants) — is an auditing standard for service providers that store, process, or transmit customer data. It is built around five **Trust Service Criteria**:

| Criterion | What it addresses |
|---|---|
| **Security** | Protection against unauthorized access |
| **Availability** | Systems available for operation and use |
| **Processing Integrity** | Processing is complete, valid, accurate, timely, authorized |
| **Confidentiality** | Confidential data is protected |
| **Privacy** | Collection, use, retention, and disposal of personal info |

| Report type | What it proves |
|---|---|
| **Type 1** | Controls are *designed* correctly — snapshot in time |
| **Type 2** | Controls *operated effectively* over 6–12 months — far more valuable |

> Enterprise customers require SOC 2 Type 2 from SaaS vendors the same way a landlord requires 12 months of bank statements — a single day's balance (Type 1) just isn't convincing.

### PCI DSS

**PCI DSS** — **P**ayment **C**ard **I**ndustry **D**ata **S**ecurity **S**tandard — applies to any organization that stores, processes, or transmits **cardholder data**. It is enforced contractually by payment card brands, not by law — but non-compliance can result in fines and loss of card-processing privileges.

- Organized into **6 categories** containing **12 core requirements**
- Smaller merchants: **self-assessment questionnaire (SAQ)**; larger merchants: assessment by a **QSA** (**Q**ualified **S**ecurity **A**ssessor)
- Requires **quarterly vulnerability scans** by **ASVs** (**A**pproved **S**canning **V**endors) and **annual penetration testing**
- Requires **segmentation testing** to verify the **cardholder data environment (CDE)** is properly isolated

---

### HIPAA

**HIPAA** — **H**ealth **I**nsurance **P**ortability and **A**ccountability **A**ct — is a U.S. law protecting **PHI** (**P**rotected **H**ealth **I**nformation). Three key rules:

| Rule | What it requires |
|---|---|
| **Privacy Rule** | Governs permissible uses and disclosures of PHI |
| **Security Rule** | Administrative, physical, and technical safeguards for electronic PHI |
| **Breach Notification Rule** | Notify HHS and affected individuals within **60 days** of discovering a breach |

---

### GDPR

**GDPR** — **G**eneral **D**ata **P**rotection **R**egulation — is the EU's comprehensive data protection law. It applies to *any* organization processing personal data of EU individuals, regardless of where that organization is located.

- Key principles: lawful basis, **data minimization**, purpose limitation, storage limitation, accuracy, security, accountability
- Breach notification to supervisory authority: within **72 hours**
- Individual rights include: access, rectification, erasure, portability, objection
- Maximum fines: **4% of annual global revenue or €20 million**, whichever is greater

> GDPR follows the data, not the company's address — a U.S. startup with EU users is fully subject to it.

---

### CIS Controls

The **CIS Controls** — developed by the **C**enter for **I**nternet **S**ecurity — are a prioritized set of **18 controls** targeting the most common and impactful attack vectors.

- Organized into **three Implementation Groups (IGs)**:
  - **IG1**: Essential controls for organizations with limited security expertise
  - **IG2**: Adds controls for organizations with moderate risk
  - **IG3**: All 18 controls for sophisticated, high-threat organizations

---

### Applying standards: gap assessment to audit

When adopting any standard, the process follows a consistent pattern:

1. **Gap assessment** — compare current controls to the standard's requirements; identify deficiencies
2. **Remediation planning** — convert gaps into a prioritized action plan with owners, timelines, and resources
3. **Evidence collection** — maintain proof that controls are implemented and effective for internal review or external audit/certification

---

### Recap

- **NIST CSF** = voluntary five-function framework (Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover) with four maturity tiers
- **NIST SP 800-171 → CMMC**: 110 requirements enforced on DoD contractors; Levels 1–3 with third-party assessment at L2/L3
- **SOC 2 Type 2** > Type 1 because it proves controls *operated effectively* over time, not just that they exist
- **GDPR** breach notification = 72 hours; **HIPAA** = 60 days
- **PCI DSS** requires quarterly ASV scans, annual pen testing, and segmentation testing

---

### Exam flags

- **SOC 2 Type 1 vs. Type 2** — know the distinction: design only (point-in-time) vs. operational effectiveness (6–12 months)
- **CMMC levels and 800-171** — know that Level 2 = all 110 SP 800-171 requirements and that L2/L3 require C3PAO assessment, not self-attestation
- **GDPR vs. HIPAA breach notification timelines** — 72 hours (GDPR) vs. 60 days (HIPAA) is a classic exam trap
