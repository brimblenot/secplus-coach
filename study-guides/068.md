## Data Types and Classifications

Data is one of an organization's most valuable assets — and knowing *what kind* of data you have determines every protection decision that follows. This lesson covers the official classification tiers (government and commercial), the major regulated data categories, and the governance roles responsible for keeping it all straight.

---

### Why classification exists

**Data classification** is the formal process of organizing data into categories based on its sensitivity and the harm that would result from unauthorized disclosure, alteration, or destruction.

- Without it, organizations either over-protect cheap data (wasting money) or under-protect critical data (creating risk)
- Regulatory frameworks almost universally *require* classification before meaningful controls can be applied
- Classification drives every downstream decision: storage, access, transmission method, and disposal

> Think of classification like a triage tag in an emergency room — it tells every responder exactly how much urgency and resource to apply, immediately, without re-debating it each time.

---

### Government classification tiers

The U.S. federal government uses a formal, ordered hierarchy for **national security information** — information whose exposure could harm the country.

| Tier | Harm from unauthorized disclosure |
|---|---|
| **Top Secret** | Exceptionally grave damage to national security |
| **Secret** | Serious damage to national security |
| **Confidential** | Damage to national security |
| **CUI** (Controlled Unclassified Information) | Sensitive but not formally classified; requires safeguarding |

- **CUI** — the current standardized federal label for sensitive-but-unclassified data; examples include law enforcement data, export-controlled technical info, and Privacy Act records
- Access to classified data requires both a **personnel security clearance** at the right level *and* a documented **need-to-know** for that specific information — clearance alone is not enough

> Think of need-to-know like a keycard plus a guest list: having the keycard (clearance) gets you into the building, but your name still has to be on the list for each room.

---

### Commercial classification tiers

Private-sector organizations use their own labels, but the structure is consistent across industries:

| Tier | Typical contents |
|---|---|
| **Restricted / Confidential** | Trade secrets, crypto keys, M&A plans, high-sensitivity PII |
| **Private / Internal** | HR records, internal policies, employee personal info |
| **Sensitive** | General business data, internal communications |
| **Public** | Press releases, marketing, published annual reports |

- The classification label directly controls: storage environment, access controls, transmission method, and destruction process

### Regulated data categories

#### PII — Personally Identifiable Information
**PII** is any data that can identify a specific individual, alone or combined with other data.

- **Direct identifiers**: SSNs, passport numbers, biometric data (fingerprints, facial geometry), financial account numbers linked to a name
- **Quasi-identifiers** — data elements that are not unique alone but can be combined to re-identify someone; research showed that zip code + date of birth + biological sex uniquely identifies ~87% of the U.S. population
- Protected under **GDPR** (EU), **CCPA** (California), and many other laws

> Quasi-identifiers are like puzzle pieces — harmless individually, but snap them together and the picture becomes unmistakable.

#### PHI — Protected Health Information
**PHI** is health information tied to an individual's past, present, or future physical or mental health condition, healthcare provision, or payment for care — when combined with any of 18 specific identifiers listed in the **HIPAA** (**Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act**) Privacy Rule.

- Vendors handling PHI on behalf of healthcare organizations must sign **BAAs** — **Business Associate Agreements** — legally binding them to apply appropriate safeguards
- Breaches carry mandatory notification requirements and significant regulatory penalties

#### Financial data
- **PAN** (**Primary Account Number**) — the credit card number; governed by **PCI DSS** (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard), which mandates encryption, access controls, network segmentation, and audit logging
- Bank account and routing numbers fall under **GLBA** (Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act)

#### Intellectual property
**Trade secrets** are information that derives economic value from not being publicly known, protected as long as the organization takes reasonable steps to keep them secret (e.g., source code, formulas, pricing algorithms). Protected by the **Defend Trade Secrets Act** and state law. **NDAs** (Non-Disclosure Agreements) are a primary contractual control.

#### Legal information
Attorney-client privileged communications, litigation strategy, and attorney work product carry heightened sensitivity — accidental disclosure can permanently waive the privilege.

- **Legal hold** — an immediate obligation to preserve all **ESI** (electronically stored information) potentially relevant to anticipated litigation; classification frameworks help identify which data custodians hold relevant ESI

#### Corporate strategic data
M&A plans, competitive intelligence, and strategic plans typically receive the highest commercial classification tier; premature disclosure can move financial markets and create insider-trading liability.

### Data ownership roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Data owner** | Senior executive; makes classification decisions, defines handling requirements, approves access |
| **Data custodian** | IT/security professional; implements and maintains technical controls specified by the data owner |

- Classification labels must be applied **at the time of creation or collection**
- Physical documents get header/footer labels; digital files get **metadata tags** readable by **DLP** (Data Loss Prevention) systems and **IRM** (Information Rights Management) platforms

---

### Handling requirements by tier

**Restricted/Confidential data** requires:
- Storage on **encrypted** systems with strict access controls and access logging
- Transmission only over encrypted channels (e.g., **TLS** — Transport Layer Security — or **VPN** — Virtual Private Network — tunnels)
- Disposal via **cryptographic erasure** or **physical media destruction**

Lower tiers carry progressively relaxed requirements; the classification label is the trigger for all of these decisions.

---

### Recap

- Government tiers go Top Secret → Secret → Confidential → CUI; clearance *plus* need-to-know is required for classified access
- Commercial tiers run Restricted → Private → Sensitive → Public; the label drives storage, access, transmission, and disposal controls
- PII, PHI, financial data, IP, legal info, and corporate strategic data each carry distinct regulatory obligations
- Data owners classify and approve; data custodians implement the controls
- Quasi-identifiers can re-identify individuals even without direct PII — combination is the risk

### Exam flags

- **Government vs. commercial classification tiers** — know the names, order, and harm thresholds (especially Top Secret vs. Secret vs. Confidential)
- **PII quasi-identifiers** — the zip + DOB + sex example and what it proves about anonymization difficulty
- **Data owner vs. data custodian** — who makes classification decisions vs. who enforces them technically
