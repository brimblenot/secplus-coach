## Agreement Types

Security professionals don't just configure firewalls — they also shape the legal relationships that govern how external parties handle organizational data and systems. This lesson covers the agreement types the Security+ exam expects you to recognize, what each one does, and what security provisions belong in each.

---

### Service Level Agreement (SLA)

An **SLA** — *Service Level Agreement* — is a contract clause or standalone document that defines the minimum acceptable performance a service provider must deliver and the remedies the customer receives when those levels aren't met.

- **Uptime** is the most common SLA metric — the percentage of time a service must be available:

| Uptime % | Permitted downtime/year |
|---|---|
| 99.9% ("three nines") | ~8.7 hours |
| 99.99% ("four nines") | ~52 minutes |
| 99.999% ("five nines") | ~5 minutes |

- Also covers: **response time** (how fast the provider acknowledges an incident), **MTTR** — *Mean Time To Repair*, the average time to fix a failure — throughput guarantees, and data recovery commitments.
- **Penalties** are usually *service credits* (discounts on future bills); severe failures may trigger *termination rights* (exit contract without penalty).
- **Security-specific SLA clauses:** breach notification timeframes, audit rights, required security controls.
- **Exclusions** often cover scheduled maintenance, customer-caused outages, and *force majeure* — unforeseeable disasters outside either party's control.

> Think of an SLA like a pizza delivery guarantee: "30 minutes or it's free." If the cloud provider's service goes down longer than allowed, you get a credit — and if it keeps happening, you can walk away.

### Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) vs. Memorandum of Agreement (MOA)

**MOU** — *Memorandum of Understanding* — is a **non-binding** document expressing mutual intent between two parties. It describes shared goals without creating enforceable legal obligations. Used for exploratory partnerships, early-stage collaborations, and preliminary information-sharing arrangements before a formal contract exists.

**MOA** — *Memorandum of Agreement* — is a **binding** document that commits each party to specific actions, deliverables, timelines, and resources. Common in government contexts: inter-agency arrangements, joint projects, shared infrastructure. Less formally drafted than a full contract, but both parties intend to be held to their commitments.

| Feature | MOU | MOA |
|---|---|---|
| Legally binding? | No | Yes |
| Level of specificity | General intent | Specific obligations |
| Typical use | Exploratory partnerships | Government/inter-agency projects |
| Can you sue for breach? | Generally no | Yes |

> An MOU is like a handshake agreement to "work together someday." An MOA is a signed project charter — both teams are on the hook.

### Master Service Agreement (MSA) and Statement of Work (SOW)

An **MSA** — *Master Service Agreement* — is a comprehensive legal contract that sets the foundational terms governing an entire ongoing business relationship. Instead of renegotiating basics on every project, both parties agree once on billing, liability, dispute resolution, IP ownership, confidentiality, and security requirements.

A **SOW** — *Statement of Work* — documents the specifics of one project or engagement within the MSA's framework: scope, deliverables, timeline, milestones, client-provided resources, and acceptance criteria.

- The MSA + SOW model is standard with consulting firms, managed service providers, and technology vendors.
- The SOW creates the written record of "what exactly was included" — critical when disputes arise.

> The MSA is the employee handbook that governs everyone working at a firm; the SOW is the specific job description for one role. You don't rewrite the handbook every time you hire.

### Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)

An **NDA** — *Non-Disclosure Agreement* — is a contract that legally obligates the receiving party to keep shared information confidential. Signed before exchanging sensitive technical, business, or financial information.

Key NDA elements:
- **Definition of confidential information** — exactly what categories are protected
- **Duration** — typically 2–5 years; may be indefinite for trade secrets
- **Permitted uses** — who inside the receiving org can access it and why
- **Protection obligations** — at least the same care the receiver uses for its own secrets
- **Remedies** — *injunctive relief* (court order to stop disclosure) + *liquidated damages* (pre-set financial compensation)

| NDA type | Who has obligations | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Unilateral (one-way) | One party only | Employee NDA at hire |
| Bilateral (mutual) | Both parties | Two companies in due diligence |
| Multilateral | Three or more parties | Consortium arrangement |

> Signing an NDA before a vendor demo is like putting a lock on a filing cabinet before letting someone browse it — they can look, but legally can't take anything out.

### Data Processing Agreement (DPA)

A **DPA** — *Data Processing Agreement* — is a contract required by **GDPR** — *General Data Protection Regulation*, the EU privacy law — whenever a **data controller** (the organization that decides why and how data is processed) shares personal data with a **data processor** (a third party that processes it on the controller's behalf). GDPR Article 28 mandates it.

Required DPA provisions (the processor must):
- Process data **only on the controller's documented instructions**
- Implement appropriate **technical and organizational security measures**
- **Notify the controller promptly** of any data breach
- Support the controller in handling **data subject rights** requests (e.g., access, deletion)
- **Delete or return** all personal data at contract end
- Disclose all **sub-processors** — third parties the processor itself hires — and get controller approval before adding new ones

> A DPA is like hiring a shredding company: the contract must specify exactly what they can touch, how they must secure it, and that every scrap comes back or gets destroyed when the job is done.

---

### Interconnection Security Agreement (ISA)

An **ISA** — *Interconnection Security Agreement* — documents the security controls and technical details governing a direct network connection between two organizations' systems. Guidance comes from **NIST SP 800-47** — *NIST Special Publication 800-47*, a federal document on managing system interconnections.

An ISA covers:
- Protocols, ports, and systems involved in the connection
- Security controls protecting each end
- Types of data transmitted
- Responsible parties and incident response contacts on both sides
- Review and reauthorization schedule

Required for: government agency-to-agency links, agency-to-contractor connections, and critical infrastructure interconnections.

> An ISA is like a building code for a shared hallway between two offices — both sides agree on the locks, fire exits, and who to call if something goes wrong.

### Security-specific contract clauses

Beyond named agreement types, security terms can be embedded in any contract. Know these:

- **Breach notification clause** — maximum time vendor has to notify the org after discovering a security incident
- **Liability and indemnification** — who pays financially when a breach occurs
- **Cyber liability insurance** requirement — vendor must maintain specified coverage
- **Data ownership clause** — confirms the org (not the vendor) owns data created during the contract
- **Asset return/destruction** — how the vendor must handle org data and assets at termination
- **Encryption, patch management, access control, monitoring** requirements — security controls embedded as contractual obligations

---

### Recap

- **SLA** sets measurable service performance targets (uptime, MTTR, breach notification) with credits or termination rights as remedies.
- **MOU** = non-binding intent; **MOA** = binding specific obligations — both used before or instead of a full contract.
- **MSA** sets framework terms once; **SOW** adds project-specific details — used together for ongoing vendor relationships.
- **NDA** legally protects confidential information shared between parties; can be unilateral, bilateral, or multilateral.
- **DPA** is legally required under GDPR when a controller shares personal data with a processor; **ISA** governs direct network interconnections between organizations.

### Exam flags

- **SLA uptime tiers** — know the three-nines / four-nines / five-nines downtime values and the ~8-hour gap between 99.9% and 99.99%.
- **MOU vs. MOA** — exam scenarios often hinge on whether an agreement is binding; MOU is not, MOA is.
- **DPA requirements under GDPR** — specifically the Article 28 obligations: processor-only-on-instructions, breach notification, sub-processor approval.
