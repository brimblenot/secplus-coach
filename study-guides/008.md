## Physical Security

Physical security controls protect facilities, equipment, and people from unauthorized access, theft, damage, and environmental hazards. Even the strongest digital defenses can be bypassed if an attacker gets hands-on with a server — they can pull hard drives, install hardware keyloggers, or simply destroy equipment.

---

### Defense-in-depth: layers from outside in

Physical security is organized as **concentric zones**, each requiring its own controls:

1. **Perimeter** — outer boundary (fencing, bollards, lighting)
2. **Building** — structure entry (vestibules, badge readers, guards)
3. **Room** — sensitive areas (server rooms, labs)
4. **Device** — individual hardware (cable locks, server cages)

---

### Perimeter controls

**Fencing** is a barrier that defines and physically enforces the outer boundary of a protected area.

- **Chain-link** — cheap, common; can be cut or climbed
- **Steel palisade** — welded steel posts; far more cut/climb resistant
- Minimum recommended height: **8 feet**
- **Razor/barbed wire** on top increases climb difficulty
- **Opaque** fencing blocks visual reconnaissance; **transparent** fencing lets cameras see out and makes climbers visible

**Bollards** are reinforced steel or concrete cylindrical posts installed at vehicle access points to stop vehicle ramming attacks.

- **Fixed** — permanently block vehicle access
- **Removable/retractable** — lower to admit authorized vehicles
- **Jersey barriers / K-rails** — larger pre-cast concrete blocks for high-security or temporary vehicle control

Think of bollards as the physical equivalent of a firewall rule that simply drops all vehicle-shaped packets.

**Security lighting** deters intruders by eliminating darkness they rely on.

- Cover all entry points, parking areas, loading docks, fence lines
- **Motion-activated lighting** conserves energy and draws immediate attention to movement
- Must be aligned with camera angles — lighting positioned to eliminate shadows enables **facial recognition** at entry points

**Security signage** is a **directive and deterrent control** — signs warning of CCTV surveillance or restricted access set behavioral expectations.

- Not a strong standalone control; reinforces other measures

### Building entry controls

**Access control vestibules** (also called **mantraps**) are small enclosures with two interlocking doors between the public exterior and secured interior — only one door can open at a time.

- Prevents **tailgating** — unauthorized person silently follows an authorized person through a secured door *without* the authorized person's knowledge
- Prevents **piggybacking** — same physical act, but *with* the authorized person's consent (a social engineering technique)
- Each person must authenticate individually before the inner door opens

Think of a mantrap like an airlock: you can't open the outer hatch until the inner hatch seals — no sneaking through in someone's wake.

**Badge readers and access card systems** are the primary electronic building access mechanism.

| Type | How it works | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **Proximity card (RFID)** — card using Radio Frequency ID signals | Communicates with reader wirelessly, no contact | Basic; no cryptography |
| **Smart card** | Embedded chip performs cryptographic operations | Stronger authentication |
| **Card + PIN** — PIN = Personal Identification Number | Two factors: something you have + something you know | Two-factor physical access |

All badge events should be **logged** to an access control database for auditing.

**Security guards** provide human judgment and physical response that automated systems cannot replicate.

- Reception: verify visitor identity, issue visitor badges, escort visitors
- Perimeter checkpoints: control vehicle/pedestrian access, inspect packages
- **Patrols**: walk prescribed routes looking for propped doors, vulnerabilities, and threats

**Two-person integrity** (also called **two-person control**) requires that no single individual is ever alone in especially sensitive areas (server rooms, vaults, classified areas).

- Prevents any one person from taking unauthorized actions without a witness
- Functions as both a physical security control and a fraud-prevention control

### Biometric access control

**Biometric systems** verify identity using unique physiological or behavioral characteristics.

- **Fingerprint readers** — scan unique ridge patterns
- **Retinal scanners** — analyze vascular pattern at the back of the eye
- **Iris scanners** — analyze the colored ring around the pupil
- **Facial recognition** — compares facial geometry to enrolled photos
- **Hand geometry readers** — measure hand and finger dimensions

Every biometric system is evaluated on three metrics:

| Metric | What it measures | High value = |
|---|---|---|
| **FAR** (False Acceptance Rate) | % of unauthorized users incorrectly authenticated | Security failure |
| **FRR** (False Rejection Rate) | % of authorized users incorrectly rejected | Operational disruption |
| **CER/EER** (Crossover/Equal Error Rate) | Point where FAR = FRR; single accuracy benchmark | **Lower = more accurate** |

FAR and FRR trade off against each other: tighten the threshold to reduce FAR and FRR rises. CER is the sweet spot and the standard comparison metric.

### Surveillance: CCTV

**CCTV** (Closed-Circuit Television) provides continuous visual monitoring and recording.

- **PTZ cameras** — Pan-Tilt-Zoom cameras that can be remotely directed to cover large areas or track subjects
- Modern **IP cameras** — network-connected cameras with built-in analytics: motion detection, license plate recognition, facial recognition
- Footage retention: typically **30–90 days**; regulated industries may require more
- Monitoring stations are most effective when **continuously staffed**

### Environmental controls

**ASHRAE** — the American Society of Heating, Refrigerating and Air-Conditioning Engineers — sets the data center environment standards:

- Temperature: **64–80°F (18–27°C)** at server air inlets
- Relative humidity: **40–60%**
  - Too high → condensation damages electronics
  - Too low → static electricity buildup damages components
- **Redundant HVAC** (Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning) systems ensure cooling continues if one unit fails

**Hot aisle / cold aisle layout** maximizes cooling efficiency by arranging server racks so all fronts (air intakes) face one direction and all rears (exhaust) face the opposite.

- **Cold aisle**: cool supply air from raised-floor vents or overhead diffusers feeds server fronts
- **Hot aisle**: hot exhaust returns to HVAC for cooling
- Prevents hot exhaust air from recirculating back into server intakes

Think of it like a cafeteria line: all trays go in one end, dirty dishes come out the other — never mixing clean and used.

### Fire suppression systems

| System | Mechanism | Data center appropriate? |
|---|---|---|
| **Wet-pipe sprinkler** | Water always in pipes; activates on heat | ❌ Water damages equipment |
| **Dry-pipe sprinkler** | Pipes empty until sprinkler opens; brief delay before water flows | Marginally better; still water |
| **Pre-action** | Requires *both* fire detector AND sprinkler activation to release water | ✅ Reduces accidental discharge |
| **Clean agent (FM-200 / Novec 1230)** | Interrupts combustion chemistry or removes heat; leaves no residue | ✅ Safe for electronics |
| **CO₂ system** | Displaces oxygen to extinguish fire | ⚠️ Lethal to humans; unmanned areas only |

- **Halon 1301** was formerly the standard clean agent but is now **banned under the Montreal Protocol** for ozone depletion — FM-200 and Novec 1230 replaced it.

---

### Physical device security

**Cable locks** anchor laptops and workstations to fixed furniture via a steel cable through a **Kensington security slot** — a standardized lock port built into most laptops — using a keyed or combination lock.

**Server cages** provide lockable enclosures within a data center that restrict access to specific racks, useful in co-location facilities where multiple organizations share a space.

---

### Recap

- Physical security is **layered**: perimeter → building → room → device; breach one and the next layer still applies
- **Vestibules/mantraps** stop tailgating (unknowing) and piggybacking (consenting social engineering) by requiring individual authentication through interlocking doors
- **CER/EER** is the single metric for comparing biometric system accuracy — **lower is better**
- **Clean agents (FM-200, Novec 1230)** replaced **Halon 1301** (banned for ozone depletion) as the data center fire suppression standard
- ASHRAE recommends **64–80°F and 40–60% humidity** for server rooms

### Exam flags

- **FAR vs. FRR vs. CER** — know what each measures, which direction is bad, and that CER is the comparison benchmark
- **Tailgating vs. piggybacking** — same physical act, different awareness of the authorized person; both defeated by vestibules
- **Fire suppression system types** — which are safe for electronics (clean agents), which are lethal (CO₂), and why Halon is no longer used
