## Impersonation

Impersonation attacks work by making you trust the wrong person — and they're the engine behind phishing, vishing, pretexting, and business email compromise. This lesson covers the major techniques, how attackers exploit psychology, and the defenses that actually work.

---

### Pretexting: building a believable cover story

**Pretexting** is the creation of a fabricated scenario (the "pretext") to justify a request that would otherwise seem suspicious.

- The attacker researches the target to make the story plausible and internally consistent
- Common pretexts: IT support fixing a "detected problem," a vendor servicing equipment, a new hire needing system access, a government auditor
- The goal is to shift the target from *"Is this person legitimate?"* to *"How do I help this person?"* — once that shift happens, most people assist without questioning

> **Analogy:** Pretexting is like a con artist wearing a plumber's uniform and saying your landlord sent them — the uniform + the story together lower your guard even when neither is verified.

---

### Elicitation: extracting information through conversation

**Elicitation** is a technique where an attacker poses as a casual acquaintance or peer and steers conversation to extract sensitive information the target would never deliberately disclose.

- No direct "give me your password" request — information surfaces naturally in what feels like networking or small talk
- Common contexts: conferences, trade shows, professional events
- Targets may volunteer internal system details, project timelines, or org structure without realizing it

> **Analogy:** Like a journalist who asks "so what's your company working on lately?" and keeps asking follow-ups — you're talking freely because it feels like a conversation, not an interrogation.

---

### Shoulder surfing: eyes on your screen

**Shoulder surfing** is physically observing a target's screen, keyboard, or documents to capture passwords, PINs, or confidential data.

- Works in coffee shops, airports, trains, open-plan offices
- Sophisticated versions use binoculars or long-range cameras from a non-threatening distance
- **Defense:** **privacy screens** — polarized film filters that make the display readable only from directly in front, appearing dark from any other angle — plus locking the screen when stepping away

> **Analogy:** Someone reading your ATM PIN over your shoulder at the bank machine — but with a laptop, it can happen 10 feet away with a camera.

---

### Dumpster diving: trash as intelligence

**Dumpster diving** is searching through an organization's discarded physical materials to recover sensitive information.

- Valuable finds: printed confidential documents, org charts, handwritten passwords, unwiped hard drives and USB drives, system manuals, calendar printouts
- **Defense:** cross-cut shredders (not strip-cut — strip shreds can be reassembled), physical destruction or **degaussing** (erasing magnetic storage with a strong magnetic field) of storage media, and a **clean desk policy** requiring employees to clear sensitive materials from desks at end of day

> **Analogy:** Think of it like dumpster diving behind a pharmacy — the pill bottles alone reveal what medications a person takes. Thrown-away org charts and printed emails are just as revealing.

---

### Tailgating vs. piggybacking: following through the door

Both exploit social norms to gain unauthorized physical access.

| Term | What happens | Target's awareness |
|---|---|---|
| **Tailgating** | Unauthorized person slips through a secured door behind an authorized person | Authorized person does **not** know |
| **Piggybacking** | Authorized person knowingly holds the door open for someone without verifying authorization | Authorized person is **aware** but complicit |

- **Defense:** **access control vestibules** (also called **mantraps**) — small enclosed areas with two doors where the first must close and lock before the second opens, physically preventing two people from passing simultaneously
- Security awareness training must establish that challenging an unrecognized person and requiring badge verification is required, not rude
- **Visitor escort policies** require all guests be accompanied by an authorized employee at all times

> **Analogy:** A mantrap is like an airlock — you can't get to the second door until the first one seals behind you, making "sliding in behind someone" physically impossible.

---

### Caller ID spoofing

**Caller ID spoofing** is the use of VoIP services — **VoIP** being internet-based phone technology — that allow an attacker to make any number appear on the recipient's caller ID, including your bank's official number or an internal company extension.

- **Defense:** Never treat caller ID as authentication. If someone calls claiming to be your bank, hang up and call the number printed on your bank card or the bank's official website.

---

### Identity fraud

**Identity fraud** occurs when an attacker uses stolen personal details — Social Security number, date of birth, address — to impersonate the victim with financial institutions or government agencies.

- Attacker may open credit cards, take out loans, access existing accounts, or file fraudulent tax returns in the victim's name
- Victim's credit and financial standing are damaged while the attacker captures the financial benefit

---

### Doppelganger domains

**Doppelganger domains** (also called **lookalike** or **homograph domains**) are domain names registered to look or sound nearly identical to a legitimate organization's domain, used for fake websites, credential harvesting, or email impersonation.

- Combined with caller ID spoofing and pretexting, these technical aids make attacks convincing even to alert targets
- **Defense:** validate identity through known, trusted channels — not through credentials the caller or email presents

---

### The helpdesk impersonation script

The most common corporate impersonation scenario: *"Hi, I'm from IT — we detected a problem with your account and need your credentials to fix it immediately."*

- Effective because IT legitimately contacts users, and the framing triggers both **authority compliance** and **urgency**
- **Defense:** firm policy that IT will **never** ask for a user's password by any channel, plus verification procedures (valid ticket number, known internal callback number) for any sensitive request

---

### Recap

- **Pretexting** builds a fabricated cover story to make a request seem legitimate; the goal is to get the target helping rather than questioning
- **Elicitation** extracts sensitive info through casual conversation, never asking directly
- **Shoulder surfing** is defeated by privacy screens; **dumpster diving** by cross-cut shredders, media degaussing, and clean desk policies
- **Tailgating** (without target's knowledge) and **piggybacking** (with target's passive cooperation) are defeated by mantraps and escort policies
- IT will **never** ask for your password — that request is always an attack

### Exam flags

- **Tailgating vs. piggybacking distinction** — exam loves testing whether the authorized person knew or didn't know
- **Access control vestibule / mantrap** as the physical defense against tailgating and piggybacking
- **Pretexting** as the technique of fabricating a scenario to justify a social engineering request
