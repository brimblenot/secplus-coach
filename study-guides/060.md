## Other Infrastructure Concepts (Domain 3)

This lesson covers the security challenges of environments outside conventional IT: IoT devices, industrial control systems, embedded systems, and operational technology. These environments matter because standard security tools — patching, antivirus, endpoint agents — often cannot be applied, yet the consequences of compromise can be physical and catastrophic.

---

### IoT devices and why they're hard to secure

**IoT (Internet of Things)** devices are network-connected hardware designed to collect data, automate physical processes, or extend digital control into the physical world — think smart thermostats, security cameras, medical monitors, industrial sensors, and smart meters.

Several properties make IoT uniquely difficult to protect:

- **Constrained resources** — limited CPU, memory, and power; cannot run full OS, endpoint agents, or heavy cryptographic operations
- **Rarely updated firmware** — manufacturers may not issue updates; users may not apply them; update mechanisms may be absent entirely
- **Default credentials** — ships with well-known combos (admin/admin) that are hardcoded or never changed
- **Decade-long lifecycles** — a sensor installed in 2010 may still run in 2030 with zero security updates
- **Diverse, low-security protocols** — **Zigbee**, **Z-Wave**, **BLE (Bluetooth Low Energy)**, **LoRaWAN**, **MQTT** — designed for efficiency, not security
- **Lateral movement risk** — one compromised device becomes a foothold into the broader network

> **Real-world anchor:** The **Mirai botnet** (2016) exploited default credentials on hundreds of thousands of IoT devices and used them to launch record-breaking **DDoS (Distributed Denial of Service)** attacks — flooding a target with traffic until it goes offline — against DNS infrastructure, taking down large swaths of the internet.

**IoT security controls:**

- **Network segmentation** into isolated **VLANs** — dedicated virtual network segments with strict firewall rules (an industrial temperature sensor has no business touching corporate finance systems)
- Inventory all devices; disable unnecessary features and services
- Change default credentials wherever possible
- Keep firmware updated when updates exist
- Monitor IoT segments for unusual traffic

### SCADA, ICS, and OT

**SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition)** is a category of industrial control software and hardware that collects real-time sensor data across large geographic areas and lets operators monitor and control physical infrastructure — power grids, water treatment, oil pipelines, manufacturing lines — from a central control room.

**ICS (Industrial Control Systems)** is the broader umbrella term. SCADA is one type of ICS. Two others:

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| **DCS** (Distributed Control Systems) | Control distributed across multiple local controllers; used in continuous processes like chemical refining |
| **PLC** (Programmable Logic Controller) | Specialized computer that directly operates individual equipment — motors, valves, pumps — via programmed logic |

**OT (Operational Technology)** is the broadest term: any hardware or software that monitors or controls physical equipment and processes.

**The critical OT vs. IT priority difference:**

| Environment | Priority order |
|---|---|
| IT | Confidentiality → Integrity → Availability |
| OT | Availability → Safety (above all) |

In OT, an unexpected shutdown of a turbine or centrifuge is not an inconvenience — it can injure or kill people. This fundamentally changes what security looks like: monthly patch cycles and antivirus scans that could interrupt a real-time control loop are often unacceptable.

> **Real-world anchor:** **Stuxnet** (2010) was malware that targeted Iranian nuclear centrifuges by exploiting vulnerabilities in Siemens **PLCs**. It manipulated centrifuge spin speeds while falsely reporting normal readings to operators — causing physical destruction without firing a single weapon.

**ICS/SCADA security controls (in priority order):**

- **Air gap** — physically isolate the OT network with no connection to IT or internet; the gold standard when operationally feasible
- **Industrial DMZ** — a middle zone hosting **historian servers** (data-aggregation systems that log OT process data) so IT and OT can exchange needed information without direct connectivity
- **Data diodes (unidirectional security gateways)** — hardware that physically enforces one-way data flow: OT data can flow *out* to IT for monitoring, but nothing can flow *back in*, making network-based attacks physically impossible
- **Whitelisted firewall rules** — permit only specific protocols, source/destination addresses, and ports
- **OT-aware network monitoring** — tools that understand industrial protocols like **Modbus**, **DNP3**, and **EtherNet/IP** and alert on anomalous commands; standard IT monitoring tools cannot interpret this traffic
- **Vendor-approved, lab-tested patches** with longer accepted cycles than IT

### Embedded systems and RTOS

**Embedded systems** are purpose-built computing devices where hardware and software are tightly integrated to perform one dedicated function — traffic controllers, medical implants, point-of-sale terminals, avionics computers. They run proprietary or stripped-down OSes; firmware may be stored in read-only memory, requiring physical access or manufacturer tools to update.

**RTOS (Real-Time Operating System)** is an OS designed for applications where *timing* is critical. Unlike standard OSes (Windows, Linux) that may delay a low-priority process, an RTOS guarantees that a task completes within a precise time window — milliseconds or microseconds. Automotive braking systems, infusion pumps, and flight control computers depend on this. Adding security monitoring or patching tools risks disrupting those timing guarantees, so security is often limited to reducing the attack surface rather than active defense.

> **Analogy:** An RTOS is like an air-traffic controller who *must* respond to every plane within a strict time window — any interruption, even a brief one, is unacceptable.

**Connected vehicles** extend these concerns: modern cars use the **CAN (Controller Area Network) bus** — designed without authentication, so any device on the bus can send commands to any other. In 2015, researchers remotely exploited a Jeep Cherokee's **Uconnect** telematics system to control steering and braking over the internet. The **OBD-II port** — the standardized physical diagnostic connector in vehicles — also provides a physical attack point.

### Recap

- IoT devices are hard to secure due to constrained resources, no updates, default credentials, and long lifecycles — segment them onto isolated VLANs
- SCADA/ICS controls physical infrastructure; compromise can cause real-world physical harm, not just data loss
- OT prioritizes **availability and safety** over confidentiality — standard IT security practices (monthly patches, AV) often cannot apply
- Air gaps and data diodes are the strongest OT isolation controls; industrial DMZs handle cases where some data exchange is required
- Embedded systems and RTOS trade security flexibility for reliability and timing determinism

### Exam flags

- **OT vs. IT priority difference** — expect a scenario asking which CIA property OT environments prioritize (availability/safety first, not confidentiality)
- **Data diodes / unidirectional gateways** — know that these physically enforce one-way data flow from OT to IT, preventing any inbound attack traffic
- **IoT default credentials and segmentation** — Mirai botnet scenario or "best control for IoT risk" questions pointing to VLAN segmentation
