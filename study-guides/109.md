## Data Roles and Responsibilities

Data governance defines who owns, protects, and processes data — and who answers when something goes wrong. The SY0-701 exam tests the specific roles in this framework, with heavy emphasis on GDPR-specific titles and the separation of duties between them.

---

### Data owner

The **data owner** is a senior business leader (not a technical staffer) who holds ultimate accountability for a specific dataset — who can access it, how it's protected, and whether its use complies with law and policy.

- Typically a C-suite or VP-level executive (e.g., CFO owns financial data, CHRO owns HR data)
- Decides *what* protections are needed; does **not** implement them personally
- Delegates day-to-day technical security to the data custodian

> Think of the data owner as a department head who sets the dress code — they decide the rules, but they don't personally check IDs at the door.

---

### Data custodian (data steward)

The **data custodian** — sometimes called the **data steward** — is the technical professional who implements the protections the data owner has decided on.

- Configures encryption, access control lists (**ACLs** — rules that define who can read or modify a resource), and backup/recovery systems
- Monitors for unauthorized access and applies data classification labels
- Has technical ability but **no authority** to override the data owner's policy decisions

> The custodian is the locksmith who installs the locks the building manager specified — they have the tools, but the manager chose the lock grade.

---

### Data controller (GDPR)

The **data controller** is a **GDPR** — *General Data Protection Regulation*, the EU's primary data protection law — term for the organization or individual that decides *why* personal data is collected and *how* it will be processed.

- Legally responsible for GDPR compliance and must document the lawful basis for every processing activity
- Must implement safeguards and respect **data subject** rights (see below)
- When sharing data with a vendor, must govern that relationship via a **Data Processing Agreement (DPA)** — a contract specifying how the vendor may handle the data

> A hospital that decides to collect patient records for treatment purposes is the data controller — it set the purpose, so it owns the legal obligation.

---

### Data processor (GDPR)

The **data processor** is a GDPR term for any organization that processes personal data *on behalf of* the data controller, following the controller's instructions.

- May **not** use the data for its own purposes beyond what the controller authorizes
- Examples: a payroll company handling employee data, a cloud storage provider storing customer files, an email marketing platform sending campaigns
- Must notify the controller of breaches on the DPA timeline, delete/return data at end of engagement, and hold any **sub-processors** to the same standards
- A DPA must exist **before** any personal data is transferred

> The data processor is a contractor hired to sort your mail — they follow your instructions and hand everything back; they can't start reading your letters for their own use.

---

### Data Protection Officer (DPO)

The **DPO** — *Data Protection Officer* — is a GDPR-mandated role for organizations that process personal data at large scale as a core activity, handle special-category data (e.g., health or biometric data) at scale, or are certain public authorities.

- Monitors GDPR compliance, advises on **Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs)** — structured reviews of privacy risk before high-risk processing begins
- Serves as the contact point for data subjects exercising their rights and for the supervisory authority
- **Cannot be dismissed or penalized** for performing DPO duties — this independence is legally protected
- May be an internal employee or an external consultant

> The DPO is like an internal ombudsman with a guaranteed contract — the organization hired them to flag problems, and it legally cannot fire them for doing exactly that.

---

### Data subject rights (GDPR)

A **data subject** is the individual whose personal data is being processed. GDPR grants them specific enforceable rights; organizations must respond within **30 days**.

| Right | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Access** | Request a copy of all personal data held about them |
| **Rectification** | Request correction of inaccurate data |
| **Erasure** ("right to be forgotten") | Request deletion when data is no longer needed or consent is withdrawn |
| **Portability** | Receive data in a machine-readable format to transfer elsewhere |
| **Object** | Opt out of certain processing, including direct marketing |

---

### Separation of duties in data governance

**Separation of duties** means no single person controls every aspect of data security end-to-end.

- **Data owner** → sets policy, approves access decisions
- **Data custodian** → implements technical controls, cannot override policy
- **Auditor** → verifies both policy and implementation, with no role in either

This three-way split ensures that misuse of data requires collusion across roles.

---

### Article 30 records of processing activities

**Article 30** of the GDPR requires organizations to maintain written records of every processing activity, documenting: purpose, categories of data and subjects, recipients, international transfer safeguards, retention periods, and security measures. These records are the primary evidence of compliance shown to regulators.

---

### Recap

- **Data owner** = accountable executive who sets policy; **data custodian** = technical implementer who enforces it — never the same person
- **Data controller** decides *why/how* data is processed; **data processor** acts on the controller's instructions — a DPA must exist between them
- The **DPO** is a legally protected, GDPR-mandated independence role for large-scale or sensitive data processors
- Data subjects have five key GDPR rights; organizations have 30 days to respond
- Separation of duties (owner → custodian → auditor) prevents any one person from having unchecked control

### Exam flags

- **Controller vs. processor distinction** — expect scenario questions where you must identify which party is the controller and which is the processor based on who decides the purpose of processing
- **DPO independence** — the fact that a DPO cannot be dismissed for performing their duties is a high-probability detail
- **Data subject rights** — know all five rights by name, especially erasure (right to be forgotten) and portability
