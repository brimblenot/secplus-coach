## Authentication, Authorization & Accounting (AAA)

AAA is the three-part framework underlying every enterprise access control system. Get this right and you have a mental scaffold for understanding how users get in, what they can do, and how you prove what happened afterward.

---

### Identification vs. authentication

**Identification** is claiming an identity — typing your username into a login screen. It is a declaration, not proof.

**Authentication** is proving the claim — supplying the correct password, fingerprint, or token. The system handles these as two separate steps.

> Analogy: Showing your ID card at a bar is identification. The bouncer comparing your face to the photo is authentication.

---

### The five authentication factors

| Factor | Category | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Something you **know** | Weakest; can be guessed or stolen | Password, PIN, passphrase, security answer |
| Something you **have** | Physical possession required | Smart card, hardware key, **OTP** (one-time password) generator, **TOTP** (time-based OTP) app |
| Something you **are** | Biometric | Fingerprint, iris/retinal scan, facial recognition, vein patterns |
| Somewhere you **are** | Location-based | Geolocation via IP or GPS |
| Something you **do** | Behavioral biometric | Keystroke dynamics, mouse patterns, handwriting |

**MFA (multi-factor authentication)** requires credentials from **at least two different categories**. A password + a TOTP code is MFA (know + have). Two passwords is *not* MFA — same category.

> Analogy: A bank vault requires both a key card AND a PIN. Stealing one is useless without the other.

---

### Authorization: what you're allowed to do

**Authorization** determines what an authenticated user may access or do. It kicks in *after* authentication succeeds.

- **Least privilege** — grant only the minimum rights needed to do the job.
- **Need-to-know** — access to specific data requires a clear business justification, even within that minimum.
- **ACL (Access Control List)** — each resource carries a list of subjects and their permitted operations (e.g., Alice: read/write; Bob: read only).
- **RBAC (role-based access control)** — permissions are assigned to *roles*, not individuals. Adding a user to the "shipping" role automatically grants all shipping permissions, so you never have to configure each resource manually per user.

> Analogy: A hotel key card is scoped to your room (RBAC by role = "guest in room 412"). The master key the manager carries is a different role with broader permissions.

---

### Accounting: the audit trail

**Accounting** (also called auditing) records what authenticated, authorized users actually did — logins, resources accessed, data transferred, logouts, source location.

- Collected via **syslog** — a standard protocol for shipping log messages from devices to a central server.
- Aggregated and analyzed by a **SIEM (Security Information and Event Management)** system, which correlates logs across sources to surface threats.
- Retained per policy and regulation to support incident response, forensics, and legal proceedings.

> Analogy: The bank's security cameras don't stop a robbery, but the footage proves who did it and how.

### AAA protocols

| Protocol | Transport & Port(s) | Encrypts | Primary Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RADIUS** (Remote Authentication Dial-In User Service) | UDP 1812 (auth/authz), UDP 1813 (accounting) | Password only | VPN, Wi-Fi 802.1X, network access |
| **TACACS+** (Terminal Access Controller Access-Control System Plus) | TCP 49 | Entire packet body | Cisco device administration |
| **DIAMETER** | TCP or **SCTP** (Stream Control Transmission Protocol — a reliable transport like TCP) | Full session | Mobile/telecom: 4G LTE, 5G |

TACACS+ is Cisco-proprietary and completely separate from older TACACS variants. DIAMETER is RADIUS's successor ("twice the radius") but is rare in enterprise IT — carriers use it.

---

### Directory services and LDAP

**LDAP (Lightweight Directory Access Protocol)** queries and modifies directory databases like **Active Directory** — the Microsoft service that stores user accounts, passwords, group memberships, and computer accounts.

- Plain LDAP: **TCP 389** (unencrypted)
- **LDAPS** (LDAP over SSL/TLS): **TCP 636** (full session encrypted)

---

### Kerberos and SSO

**Kerberos** is the ticket-based authentication protocol used inside Active Directory (and many Linux environments), running on **TCP/UDP port 88**. Instead of re-sending your password to every service, you prove yourself once to the **KDC (Key Distribution Center)** and get a **TGT (Ticket Granting Ticket)**. Each time you need a service, the KDC's **TGS (Ticket Granting Service)** exchanges your TGT for a short-lived service ticket. The password never travels across the network again.

**SSO (Single Sign-On)** is the pattern where one authentication grants access to many systems. Kerberos in AD is SSO. It prevents **credential fatigue** — the tendency to pick weak passwords when juggling too many accounts.

> Analogy: Kerberos is like a theme-park wristband: prove you paid once at the gate, then wave your wrist at every ride.

---

### Federation and federated identity protocols

**Federation** extends SSO *across organizations*. An **identity provider (IdP)** vouches for its users; a **service provider (SP)** trusts that voucher and grants access without requiring a second account.

| Protocol | What it is | Primary use |
|---|---|---|
| **SAML** (Security Assertion Markup Language) | XML standard for exchanging auth/authz data between IdP and SP | Enterprise web-app SSO |
| **OAuth** (Open Authorization) | Framework for *delegated authorization* — lets an app act on your behalf without your credentials | "Login with Google" granting limited account access |
| **OIDC** (OpenID Connect) | Identity layer built on top of OAuth 2.0 that adds authentication | SSO across web apps |

> Analogy: Federation is like a TSA PreCheck membership: your home airport (IdP) vetted you, and other airports (SPs) accept that vetting without re-screening you from scratch.

---

### Recap

- **AAA = Authenticate** (prove who) → **Authorize** (decide what) → **Account** (record everything).
- MFA requires two *different* factor categories; same-category combos don't count.
- RADIUS encrypts only the password (UDP); TACACS+ encrypts the full packet (TCP 49) and is used for device admin.
- Kerberos issues tickets so passwords never traverse the network after login; this is the foundation of AD SSO.
- SAML handles enterprise federation; OAuth handles delegated authorization; OIDC adds authentication on top of OAuth.

### Exam flags

- **RADIUS vs. TACACS+** port numbers, transport (UDP vs. TCP), and encryption scope — a favorite comparison question.
- **MFA factor categories** — expect scenarios asking whether a given combo qualifies as true MFA.
- **Kerberos ticket flow** (TGT → TGS → service ticket) and its port (88).
