## Mobile Device Vulnerabilities

Mobile devices are high-value targets: they cross network boundaries constantly, carry sensitive data, and stay persistently connected to the internet. This lesson covers the major attack surfaces — from OS tampering to Bluetooth exploits to SIM fraud — and the management frameworks used to control them.

---

### Jailbreaking and rooting

**Jailbreaking** is the process of removing Apple's built-in software restrictions on iOS devices. iOS enforces a closed ecosystem: every app must come from the App Store and be code-signed by Apple. Jailbreaking exploits kernel vulnerabilities to bypass these controls.

- Enables installing apps outside the App Store (no security review)
- Disables code-signing and the **app sandbox** — the isolation layer that keeps apps from touching each other or the OS
- Can bypass **MDM** — Mobile Device Management — controls (covered below)
- May weaken the **Secure Enclave** — Apple's dedicated security chip that guards biometric data and cryptographic keys
- MDM can detect jailbroken status via integrity checks and block corporate access

**Rooting** is the Android equivalent: gaining full root-level administrator access. Android is more open, so rooting is generally easier. Users often install custom **ROMs** — replacement operating systems — which may lack official security patches or have **SELinux** (Android's mandatory access-control system) disabled. Android's **fragmentation** problem — many manufacturers are slow to push OS updates for older models — means large numbers of production devices run OS versions with known, unpatched vulnerabilities.

> *Analogy: Jailbreaking/rooting is like picking the lock on a bank vault door, then propping it open permanently — everything inside is now accessible to whoever walks in.*

---

### Sideloading

**Sideloading** is installing an application from a source other than the official platform app store, bypassing that store's security review entirely.

- **Android:** Sideloading is on by default via **APK files** — Android application package files installed directly; users must grant "install from unknown sources" permission, which many do carelessly
- **iOS:** Sideloading is blocked without jailbreaking; narrow exceptions exist for enterprise distribution and **TestFlight** (Apple's beta-testing platform)
- Sideloaded apps may contain malware, hidden functionality, or excessive permission requests that would fail official review

> *Analogy: Sideloading is like buying food from an unlicensed street cart — it skipped every health inspection the official restaurant had to pass.*

### Malicious applications

Even apps that pass official store review can be malicious. Common tactics and behaviors:

| Behavior | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Permission harvesting** | Requests far more permissions than the app needs (e.g., a flashlight app requesting contacts, SMS, microphone, camera, precise location) |
| **Premium SMS fraud** | Silently subscribes the victim to paid text-message services |
| **Banking trojans** | Monitor for financial apps; overlay fake login screens to steal credentials |
| **Ad fraud** | Generate fake ad clicks to profit from advertising networks |
| **Dynamic code loading** | Passes review behaving innocuously, then downloads malicious code later |

The **Pegasus** spyware (NSO Group) exploited iOS **zero-click vulnerabilities** — no user interaction needed — to surveil journalists and officials worldwide. The **Stagefright** family of Android vulnerabilities allowed code execution just by receiving a crafted multimedia message, before the recipient even opened it. These demonstrate that even careful users can be compromised when the platform itself is the target.

### Bluetooth attacks

**Bluetooth** is the short-range wireless protocol connecting headsets, keyboards, and peripherals. Three key attack types:

| Attack | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Bluejacking** | Sends unsolicited messages to nearby Bluetooth devices — nuisance only, no data theft |
| **Bluesnarfing** | Exploits the **OBEX Push Protocol** (a Bluetooth file-transfer protocol) to silently read the victim's contacts, messages, calendar, and files |
| **Bluebugging** | Full remote control of the target — attacker can make calls, send texts, and access data |

**Blueborne** (2017) was a family of Bluetooth protocol-stack flaws across multiple OSes allowing unauthenticated code execution and worm-like spread between paired — or even unpaired — devices in range, requiring zero user interaction. **Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)** — the low-power variant used in fitness trackers, medical devices, and IoT sensors — introduces its own separate vulnerability surface. Disabling Bluetooth when not in use eliminates the attack surface entirely.

> *Analogy: Bluejacking is a stranger shouting at you. Bluesnarfing is them picking your pocket. Bluebugging is them taking the wheel of your car.*

### NFC attacks

**NFC** (Near Field Communication) is the very-short-range wireless technology behind contactless payments and access cards.

- **Relay attack:** Two attacker-controlled devices extend the effective NFC range. Device 1 sits near the victim's real card and captures the NFC signal; it relays that signal over a longer channel to Device 2, positioned at a payment terminal — completing a fraudulent transaction while the card never leaves the victim's pocket.
- **Card emulation attack:** A device is programmed to impersonate a legitimate card using previously captured card data.

> *Analogy: A relay attack is like two thieves with walkie-talkies — one stands next to you at the coffee shop while the other stands at an ATM miles away, and your card "magically" authorizes a withdrawal.*

---

### Wi-Fi risks

Mobile devices automatically reconnect to known network names (**SSIDs** — Service Set Identifiers, the human-readable names of wireless networks).

- **Evil twin access point:** An attacker creates a Wi-Fi hotspot with the same SSID as a network the victim previously used. The device auto-connects and routes all traffic through the attacker.
- **Open Wi-Fi:** Airports, hotels, and coffee shops transmit unencrypted traffic in the clear; captive portal pages can serve malicious content.
- Using a **VPN** (Virtual Private Network — encrypts all traffic between device and a trusted server) on untrusted networks mitigates most Wi-Fi risks.

### SS7 vulnerabilities and SIM swapping

**SS7** (Signaling System 7) is the decades-old protocol that routes calls and SMS across global telecom infrastructure. Its vulnerabilities allow attackers with access to the signaling network — typically governments, criminal groups, or telecom insiders — to intercept SMS and track location. This directly undermines **SMS-based MFA** (Multi-Factor Authentication), since SS7 flaws mean an SMS "for your eyes only" may not be.

**SIM swapping** (also called **SIM hijacking**) is a social engineering attack targeting the carrier, not the device:

1. Attacker gathers victim's personal info (social media, data breaches)
2. Calls carrier, impersonates victim, convinces a rep to move victim's number to attacker's SIM
3. Attacker now receives all calls and SMS — including MFA codes for banks, email, and other accounts

**Defenses:**
- Set a **carrier-level SIM lock/PIN** — a code the carrier must verify before processing any SIM change
- Replace SMS-based MFA with an authenticator app or hardware security key

> *Analogy: SIM swapping is like a con artist walking into the DMV, impersonating you, and getting your license plate transferred to their car — now every ticket and toll bill (and every authentication code) goes to them.*

### Grayware and stalkerware

**Grayware** refers to apps that are not overtly malicious but engage in privacy-invasive behavior: harvesting and selling excessive data, showing intrusive ads, or using manipulative design to extract value from users without their full understanding.

**Stalkerware** is a subset: commercial apps marketed for monitoring a partner or child that operate covertly, hide from the app list, and continuously upload location, messages, and other data to whoever purchased access. These are increasingly recognized as domestic-abuse tools, and major platforms have adopted policies restricting their distribution.

### Mobile Device Management (MDM)

**MDM** (Mobile Device Management) is the enterprise technology platform that enforces security policy across an organization's fleet of mobile devices.

MDM capabilities include:

- Enforcing **full-disk encryption** and screen-lock PIN complexity
- Restricting app sources and deploying approved corporate apps
- **Remote wipe** — selectively erasing only corporate data, or fully wiping the device, if it's lost or stolen
- Detecting jailbroken/rooted devices and blocking their corporate access
- Enforcing VPN use and **geofencing** — policies that restrict actions based on device location

**MAM** (Mobile Application Management) is a subset of MDM that manages only applications and their data, leaving the rest of the personal device untouched — especially relevant in **BYOD** scenarios.

| Deployment model | Who owns the device | IT control level |
|---|---|---|
| **COPE** (Corporate-Owned, Personally Enabled) | Organization | Full control |
| **BYOD** (Bring Your Own Device) | Employee | Limited MDM management |
| **CYOD** (Choose Your Own Device) | Organization (employee picks from approved list) | Full control |

> *Analogy: MDM is like a corporate IT department that can remotely deadbolt certain rooms in your house, check which doors are unlocked, and — if you lose your key — erase the whole floor plan.*

---

### Recap

- **Jailbreaking (iOS) / Rooting (Android)** removes OS security controls, bypasses app sandboxing, and can defeat MDM — detected through integrity checks
- **Sideloading** skips app store security review; default-on for Android APKs, blocked on iOS without jailbreak
- **Bluejacking = nuisance; Bluesnarfing = data theft via OBEX; Bluebugging = full remote control**
- **SIM swapping** socially engineers the carrier to redirect your number — defeats SMS-based MFA; counter with a carrier PIN and switch to authenticator apps
- **MDM** enforces encryption, remote wipe, app control, and jailbreak detection; COPE/BYOD/CYOD differ in ownership and control level

### Exam flags

- **Bluetooth attack distinctions:** Bluejacking vs. Bluesnarfing vs. Bluebugging — expect scenario questions requiring you to name the correct attack from a description
- **SIM swapping mechanics and defenses:** How it bypasses SMS MFA and what stops it (carrier PIN, authenticator apps)
- **MDM deployment models:** COPE vs. BYOD vs. CYOD — know which offers highest IT control and what trade-offs each represents
