## Security Procedures (Domain 5)

Security procedures are the *how* of a security program — step-by-step documented instructions for performing specific security tasks. This lesson covers the major procedure types you'll see on the exam and why each one matters operationally.

---

### Policies vs. standards vs. procedures

These three terms are frequently confused. Know the exact distinction:

- **Policy** — states management's intent; answers *what must be done and why*
- **Standard** — specifies measurable requirements; answers *what exactly must be used or configured*
- **Procedure** — documents step-by-step instructions; answers *how a task is actually performed*

Procedures matter for three reasons: **consistency** (same steps every time), **training** (a new hire can perform a task correctly from day one), and **audit evidence** (an auditor needs proof that a policy is actually followed).

---

### Change management procedures

A **change management procedure** is the documented process for submitting, reviewing, approving, implementing, and recording any change to a system, application, or configuration.

Key steps in order:

1. **RFC** (**Request for Change** — a form capturing the proposed change) is submitted with: description, business justification, affected systems, timeline, risk assessment, testing plan, and rollback plan
2. **CAB** (**Change Advisory Board** — a committee of technical and business stakeholders) reviews and approves or rejects the RFC
3. Change is implemented during a **maintenance window** — a scheduled low-impact time slot
4. **Verification** confirms the change worked and caused no new problems
5. Everything is logged in the change management system for a complete, auditable history

> Think of the CAB like a code-review committee: no change ships until stakeholders sign off, and every merge is logged permanently.

### Onboarding procedures

An **onboarding procedure** defines every step to set up a new employee securely before they touch a production system.

Key steps:
- HR notifies IT → account created in **Active Directory** (or **Azure Active Directory** — Microsoft's cloud identity service) → email provisioned → role-based application access granted
- **Principle of least privilege** governs access: the new hire gets only what their specific job requires — nothing more
- Device setup sequence: naming convention → **MDM** (**Mobile Device Management** — platform that centrally manages and enforces policies on enrolled devices) enrollment → **BitLocker** (**BitLocker** — Windows full-disk encryption) enabled → software installed → endpoint protection deployed
- Employee completes **security awareness training** and signs the **AUP** (**Acceptable Use Policy** — a document defining permitted and prohibited uses of organizational systems) before accessing sensitive systems

> Like a new employee badge: it's activated only after HR paperwork clears, and it only opens the doors that person's role actually needs.

---

### Offboarding and termination procedures

**Offboarding procedures** define how to safely remove a departing employee's access and recover assets. This is among the most security-critical procedures because unrevoked access is a direct attack surface.

- **Voluntary departure:** procedure initiated on or before the last day
- **Involuntary termination:** access revoked *immediately* — often before the employee is even notified
- Checklist includes: disable AD account → revoke **MFA** (**Multi-Factor Authentication**) tokens and app passwords → invalidate **API keys** and **OAuth tokens** (credentials used by apps and services) → remove cloud and VPN access → collect laptop, badge, hardware tokens, keys → preserve email and files before any deletion
- **Legal hold** — if litigation is anticipated, all data tied to that employee must be preserved without modification until legal proceedings conclude
- Exit interview includes a reminder of ongoing obligations under any **NDA** (**Non-Disclosure Agreement**)

> Terminating access immediately is like rekeying the locks the moment someone leaves — waiting even a day is a window an attacker can exploit.

### Patch management procedures

A **patch management procedure** is the step-by-step process for finding, evaluating, testing, approving, deploying, and confirming fixes for known vulnerabilities.

Steps in order:
1. **Identify** patches via automated scanners, vendor advisories, and mailing lists
2. **Triage** using **CVSS** (**Common Vulnerability Scoring System** — a 0–10 scale rating vulnerability severity) score, exploitability, system criticality, and active exploitation reports
3. **Test** in a representative test environment that mirrors production to catch compatibility issues before they affect live systems
4. **Submit through change management** before any production deployment
5. **Deploy** using automated tools (e.g., **WSUS** — **Windows Server Update Services**, Microsoft's built-in patch distribution tool; or **Ansible** — an open-source configuration management and automation platform)
6. **Verify** by rescanning to confirm the vulnerability is closed

Typical remediation **SLAs** (**Service Level Agreements** — formal deadlines for completing a task):

| Severity | Remediation deadline |
|----------|----------------------|
| Critical | 15 days |
| High | 30 days |
| Medium | 90 days |
| Low | 180 days |

Patches that *cannot* be applied must be documented as **exceptions** with a business reason, residual risk assessment, and **compensating controls** (alternative measures that reduce risk when the primary control cannot be applied).

> Patch management is like a safety recall: you track every affected vehicle (system), fix them by a deadline, and document any that can't be serviced — along with what you're doing instead.

### Password reset procedures

A **password reset procedure** defines how to verify a user's identity before changing their credentials. Without it, an attacker can call the help desk, impersonate a legitimate user, and take over the account — a classic **social engineering** attack.

Acceptable identity verification methods:
- Call back on a phone number already in the HR/IT system
- Manager confirms the request
- In-person verification with a government-issued photo ID

After verification: create a temporary password meeting complexity policy → flag the account to **require a password change on next login** → document the event in the ticketing system (who requested, who performed, method used, timestamp).

> The callback rule is like a bank calling you back on your number on file before discussing your account — if someone else called in pretending to be you, they can't receive that call.

---

### Backup and recovery procedures

**Backup procedures** specify how data is backed up; **recovery procedures** specify how it is restored.

Backup procedure covers: backup type per system, schedule, retention period, destination storage, and encryption requirements.

Verification steps:
- **Automated integrity checks** confirm each backup completed without corruption
- **Restore testing** — actually restoring data to a test environment to confirm it is complete and functional (not just assumed to be)

Recovery procedure covers: who has authority to declare a disaster and initiate recovery, the order systems are restored based on **business criticality**, and communication responsibilities.

> A backup you've never tested is like a fire extinguisher you've never checked — it may look fine and do nothing when you need it.

---

### Vulnerability management procedures

**Vulnerability management procedures** govern the schedule for scanning, remediation deadlines (the SLA table above applies here too), and the **exception handling process** for systems that cannot be patched on time.

---

### Access review procedures

**Access review procedures** define a periodic process to confirm that current access rights are still appropriate and revoke any that are not.

- **Quarterly access certification reviews** — system owners or managers review user access lists and confirm or revoke each entry
- Any access that cannot be confirmed as still appropriate is **revoked promptly**

> Like auditing who still has a key to your office every quarter — if you can't justify why someone has it, you change the lock.

---

### Physical security procedures

Physical procedures cover:

- **Visitor management** — sign-in requirements and mandatory escort policies (visitors must be accompanied at all times in restricted areas)
- **Badge issuance and replacement** processes
- **Clean desk inspections** — periodic checks that sensitive materials are not left exposed
- **Reporting process** for physical incidents such as **tailgating** (an unauthorized person following an authorized person through a controlled entry) or discovery of unauthorized individuals in restricted areas

---

### Recap

- Procedures answer *how* a task is performed; policies answer *what/why*; standards answer *what exactly*
- Change management requires an RFC → CAB review → implementation → verification → documented record
- Offboarding must revoke access **immediately** for involuntary terminations; a legal hold freezes data if litigation is anticipated
- Patch SLAs: Critical = 15 days, High = 30, Medium = 90, Low = 180; exceptions require documentation and compensating controls
- Password reset procedures exist specifically to defeat social engineering of the help desk

---

### Exam flags

- **Scenario: someone calls the help desk claiming to be a user and asks for a password reset** — the correct control is identity verification (callback on registered number, manager confirmation, or photo ID) before any reset is performed
- **Scenario: an employee is involuntarily terminated** — access revocation must happen *immediately*, before or at the moment of notification, not at end of day
- **Scenario: a patch cannot be applied within the required SLA** — the correct response is a formal exception with documented business reason, residual risk assessment, and compensating controls
